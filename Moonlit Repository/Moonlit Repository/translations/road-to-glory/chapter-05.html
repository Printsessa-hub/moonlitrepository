ROAD TO GLORY · Chapter 05

Chapter 5

“I thought Second Aunt had already exhausted herself worrying about my affairs, so there’s no need for her to concern herself with my younger sister’s marriage.” As soon as Ji Wanqing spoke these words, the room fell suddenly silent.

“The Marquis of Jingbei’s household has raised us for many years. Though the household is not what it once was, as daughters of the Ji family, it is only natural that we should do our small part to help.”

Ji Wanqing raised the corners of her lips slightly, revealing a hint of sarcasm, and said calmly, “However, my father lost his life upholding our family’s honor. He now has only two children left in this world. My younger sister has a gentle and delicate disposition, so I hope Grandmother will show her greater consideration.”

Having said this, she turned her gaze directly toward the head of the table, where her grandmother, Madam He, had remained silent the entire time.

Lady Cao was a rather blunt and unscrupulous woman; speaking with someone like her was exhausting. Ji Wanqing hoped to resolve this matter decisively and efficiently; these few words were all meant for Madam He’s ears.

Her words were veiled, yet her meaning was clear: she was well aware that the household had schemes involving the Empress, and she wasn’t opposed to cooperating—but on one condition: the household must not interfere in Ji Wanxiang’s marriage.

Ji Wanqing’s gaze was unwavering. Though she was outnumbered and weak, if she really went all out to resist, it would be easy for things to fall apart.

Since she couldn’t escape the matter involving Empress Ji, she might as well use it to her advantage. Whether the benefits were great or small, she was certain the other party could weigh the pros and cons clearly.

Since family ties were now useless, she would speak directly of interests. If neither she nor her sister could live well, what was there left to hesitate about? At worst, she would simply follow her parents and elder brother in death.

Ji Wanqing had always borne a slight resemblance to her father in her features, and now her gaze was the spitting image of his. For a fleeting moment, Madam He was dazed, as if her eldest son, the one who had brought her such immense pride and who was so strong-willed, were standing right before her.

She snapped back to her senses and nodded. “In that case, let us put this matter to rest.”

Having served as a Marquis’s wife for decades, Madam He possessed a sharp eye. Since her eldest granddaughter had already gone all out, she acted decisively, making the decision herself before Lady Cao could interject.

Setting aside the fact that she hadn’t yet agreed to Lady Cao’s request, a simple comparison of the two situations made it clear that Empress Ji’s position was far more important.

Madam He turned her gaze toward Lady Cao and ordered bluntly, “Drop this matter. Otherwise, I’ll have your daughter marry into that family.”

Lady Cao seethed with indignation, but she ultimately feared her mother-in-law. After a long moment of reluctance, she reluctantly agreed. The loving facade she usually put on had vanished completely, and she glared fiercely at Ji Wanqing.

In all these past decades, she had never realized her niece possessed such capabilities.

Ji Wanqing didn’t mind; now that the mask had been torn away, none of that mattered anymore. Having secured Madam He’s consent, she finally felt at ease.

Now was the time to press her advantage. Without a moment’s pause, Ji Wanqing continued, “Grandmother, please allow me to speak: when my father was alive, he had already arranged a marriage for my younger sister. Now that the mourning period is over, it is the perfect time for her to be married off.”

Madam He looked at her with a puzzled expression, scrutinizing her for a while before finally nodding. “Since it was arranged by your father in the past, then let it be done as you say.”

As for the fact that the two families had not exchanged tokens of betrothal, or that the Zheng family had fallen on hard times—she made no mention of any of it.

It was indeed easier to speak with someone whose mind was clear. Having achieved her goal, Ji Wanqing rose as if nothing had happened and, just as she had done in the past, curtsied properly. “Please allow your granddaughter to take her leave, so as not to disturb your rest, Grandmother.”

Madam He also returned to her usual self, even appearing a bit more amiable. “Go ahead. You’ve had a tiring day today; have your meal early and get some rest.”

Ji Wanqing remained composed. Ignoring Lady Cao, who was gritting her teeth in fury, she turned and walked away at a steady, measured pace.

　　*

As soon as she received Madam He’s permission, Ji Wanqing immediately sent Granny He out to notify the Zheng family, instructing Mrs. Zheng to bring Zheng Yi to perform the Six Rites.

This time, she did not send someone secretly; instead, she ordered Granny He to lead the party in a carriage, exiting through the side gate with great fanfare.

Before long, everyone in the mansion—from the masters to the servants—knew that the Second Young Lady was to marry a military officer from a humble background. It was said that this match had been arranged by the late Marquis; the Grand Madam was simply following his wishes, and once the Second Young Lady had completed her mourning period, the two families would proceed with the engagement.

In these times, it was not well-regarded for a young lady to be assertive. Moreover, to guard against any unexpected changes, Ji Wanqing first pinned a serious label on Madam He and then invoked her late father’s name—all to prevent either of them from going back on their word.

Her father had been the previous head of the family; if he had arranged a marriage for his daughter, neither her biological mother nor the succeeding Marquis could easily retract it.

This was a perfect opportunity; as long as Yanshou Hall did not deny it, the matter would be considered settled. This move not only did no harm to Ji Wanxiang’s reputation as a lady of the household but also reinforced the Ji family’s reputation for honoring their word and keeping their promises.

Ji Wanqing snorted coldly, thinking that her so-called elders were getting off easy.

However, until the matter was settled, Ji Wanqing remained uneasy. She instructed Granny He to convey her message: the Zheng family must hasten to complete the Six Rites and welcome her younger sister into the family as soon as possible.

With such haste, the wedding would inevitably be rushed, which might be unfair for Ji Wanxiang. But there was no other choice; for the marriage to proceed smoothly was already the greatest hope both sisters could have.

The Zheng family understood immediately, and since Zheng Yi had to rush to the border city, there was no time to delay. The very next day happened to be an auspicious date. That morning, Mrs. Zheng hurriedly prepared tea, wine, and other items; by the afternoon, she was leading the matchmaker through the main gate of the Marquis of Jingbei’s mansion to propose the marriage.

As Ji Wanqing and her sister were unmarried women, they could not be involved in these matters, so Lady Cao received Mrs. Zheng.

Lady Cao was slow and indecisive, speaking in a sarcastic manner. However, Madam He had warned her in advance, and she also feared that Ji Wanqing might truly take drastic measures—a situation the household could not account for to the Empress. In the end, she accepted the betrothal gift and agreed to the Zheng family’s proposal.

Mrs. Zheng had long been mentally prepared for this; she took no notice of Lady Cao’s expressions. With the betrothal gift accepted, she breathed a sigh of relief and returned two days later to inquire about the bride’s name.

Lady Cao reluctantly handed over her second niece’s birth chart. Mrs. Zheng snatched it up, opened it on the spot, and scrutinized it to ensure its accuracy.

Ji Wanqing had already instructed Granny He to convey her younger sister’s birth chart. She feared that Lady Cao might be confused or make trouble; if the wrong birth chart were sent, the engagement could be called off.

Fortunately, Lady Cao wasn’t that foolish.

Mrs. Zheng breathed a sigh of relief and hurried home, placing Ji Wanxiang’s birth chart before the ancestral altar to seek guidance on its auspiciousness or inauspiciousness.

This was certainly an auspicious sign. As soon as the betrothal ceremony was completed, Mrs. Zheng wasted no time in preparing the betrothal gifts and delivering them without delay to the Marquis of Jingbei’s mansion. After the grand ceremony, she invited the matchmaker to her home to set the wedding date.

The wedding procession was set for one month later.

The speed at which the Six Rites were completed was unprecedented; in just twelve days, the Six Rites had been reduced to five. By this point, with both the betrothal letter and the ceremonial documents in hand, Ji Wanxiang was, strictly speaking, already a member of the Zheng family.

The Ji sisters, who had been eagerly awaiting this moment, breathed a sigh of relief. Ji Wanqing now had her hands free to devote herself entirely to packing her younger sister’s dowry.

With Ji Wanxiang’s wedding imminent, Lady Cao still showed no sign of action; it seemed unlikely that the clan would contribute to the dowry. However, this did not matter; while their parents were still alive, they had saved up a complete set of dowry items for the sisters, all of which were ready and in Ji Wanqing’s possession.

Speaking of these dowry items, there was a bit of a complication involved.

After Ji Wanqing’s father passed away, her second uncle inherited the title and ancestral estate. However, the rewards her father had received for years of military service, along with her mother’s dowry, were not included in the inheritance; these belonged solely to the two sisters.

Yet there was a dilemma: at that time, young women of noble families were not permitted to hold private property in their own names. By convention, all these funds and possessions would be entrusted to their grandmother, Madam He, or their second aunt, Lady Cao, for safekeeping.

Only after the sisters were married would they be included in their dowries and taken with them upon their marriage.

The amount of wealth involved was immense, and it was hard to say how much would remain if entrusted to others. Moreover, wealth can tempt the heart; once the exact amount is known, an overly greedy person might even go so far as to have Ji Wanqing and her sister “die of illness” to pocket the fortune for themselves.

That way, they could legitimately seize the assets.

How could Ji’s mother possibly accept this? Just a few days after falling ill, she sensed something was amiss and immediately wrote a letter to send out of the capital to her parents’ home.

She was the only daughter of the second wife, yet it was her older brother, born to the first wife, who inherited the family estate. Their sibling relationship was strained, and with the distance between them, they rarely kept in touch. However, knowing her brother’s character, she was certain he would step in at this critical moment.

Ji’s mother held on with her last breath until her brother arrived. After her death, thanks to her uncle’s intervention, the property passed directly into Ji Wanqing’s hands, where it has remained to this day.

There were several large estates on the outskirts of the capital, all situated in excellent locations with fertile land. There were also gold and silver jewelry, ornaments, hairpins and bracelets, antiques, calligraphy and paintings—in short, everything one could possibly need.

Ji Wanqing divided these possessions into two equal shares, one for each sister. Since her younger sister would have to leave the capital after marriage and did not know when she would be able to return, she made some adjustments accordingly. She gave her fewer bulky items and more banknotes, valuables, and other easily portable belongings.

Before their deaths, their parents had accumulated a considerable amount of high-quality timber, intending to have furniture made once the sisters became formally engaged. Now, however, there was certainly no time for that. So she divided the timber in half and included one portion as Ji Wanxiang's dowry. In the future, when Ji Wanxiang had a daughter of her own, the wood could still be put to good use.

As they carefully went through the dowry items one by one, the sisters could not help but break down in tears and embrace each other. Everything contained traces of their parents' love and care.

Ji Wanqing busied herself with preparations, working tirelessly. It was not until two days before the wedding that she finally managed to get everything in order. Only then did she breathe a sigh of relief.

There were still two days left. She was just about to give her younger sister some heartfelt advice when, unexpectedly, a major event occurred.

On the day before the wedding, the schemes of Empress Ji and Marquis Jingbei finally came to light. The strange things that Ji Wanqing had sensed all along finally had an explanation.

An imperial edict was issued, decreeing that Ji Wanqing was to marry the current Crown Prince.
