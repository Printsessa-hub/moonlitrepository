ROAD TO GLORY · Chapter 01

Chapter 1

Brief Summary:

Ji Wanqing finds herself reincarnated as the legitimate daughter of a marquis’s household—though, unfortunately, she is an orphan. She is thrust into a marriage by her aunt, the Step-Empress (继皇后), to the Crown Prince, the son of the Empress Yuan.

Everyone knew that the Step-Empress and her son are plotting to seize the throne, and as a thorn forcibly planted in the Eastern Palace, her situation is truly precarious. However, this does nothing to hinder Ji Wanqing from becoming the sole favorite of the Eastern Palace and steadily rising through the ranks.

***

The sky was thick with clouds, tinged with a faint haze. It was the season when migratory birds flew south, and a cool breeze swept by, bringing a chill in the air.

The majestic imperial palace towered overhead, its vermilion walls and golden glazed tiles exuding an indescribable air of solemnity and grandeur.

The long palace corridor winds its way in silence. Two figures walked in silence, one ahead and one behind: the one in front was a palace attendant leading the way, while the one behind was a young girl dressed in elegant lake-blue robes.

The young girl was about fifteen or sixteen years old, with slender shoulders, a narrow waist, and a tall, slender figure. She had delicate eyebrows and almond-shaped eyes, a fair complexion, and rosy cheeks—her appearance was strikingly beautiful. She followed silently behind the palace attendant, neither hurrying nor lagging behind.

Her surname was Ji, and her given name was Wanqing; she was the legitimate daughter of the Marquis of Jingbei.

Ji Wanqing raised her eyes slightly and glanced at the gray-green figure seven or eight steps ahead. She had been walking for nearly half an hour; it was about time they arrived.

She had been summoned by the Empress and was now on her way to the palace for an audience. Palace regulations were strict: only high-ranking imperial concubines were permitted to ride in palanquins; daughters of officials, such as Ji Wanqing, who had been summoned for an audience, could only proceed on foot.

Fortunately, she was in good physical shape from her daily exercise; otherwise, she truly wouldn’t have been able to walk the entire way without breaking a sweat or losing her breath.

The two turned a corner, walked a bit further, and arrived at the entrance to Kunning Palace. The young eunuch on guard saw them and dashed inside to announce their arrival.

The palace attendant leading the way paused and turned around. “Miss Ji, please wait a moment.”

The palace attendant spoke in a soft, gentle voice, and her every movement was impeccably proper; even the curve of the smile at the corner of her mouth seemed precisely measured. Though polite and respectful, there was an inescapable sense of detachment.

Ji Wanqing understood immediately that the attendant was not being warm—her words and actions were merely instinctive. She said nothing, merely nodding to indicate she understood.

The two exchanged no further words. Ji Wanqing raised her eyes and, without betraying her thoughts, surveyed the magnificent palace before her, with its double eaves and soaring ridges.

It spanned nine bays wide, with vermilion-painted partition screens and massive red-lacquered columns too thick for a single person to encircle, the central front of the main hall displayed a plaque with a blue background and red border. Inscribed upon it in three large, flowing characters in gold lacquer were the words “Kunning Palace.”

It was said to have been written by the founding Emperor, and this palace had been the residence of empresses throughout the dynasties—the mothers of the nation.

Ji Wanqing lowered her eyelids, concealing a trace of sarcasm in her eyes.

Now, the mistress of Kunning Palace also bore the surname Ji; by family hierarchy, she should address her as “Aunt.”

In the past, when her parents were still alive, she had been their cherished daughter—the beloved, eldest daughter of the marquis’s household, born of the legitimate line—yet she had never once set foot inside Kunning Palace. Now that her parents and elder brother had passed away in their prime, leaving her an orphan, she had the good fortune to be specially summoned by Empress Ji.

　　*

Ji Wanqing possessed memories from two lifetimes. In her previous life, she had been a modern woman who, after dying in an accident, was reborn as a tiny infant while retaining her memories.

Her father was the Marquis of Jingbei, who had held his title for several years, and her mother was the Marchioness, who managed the household affairs. From the moment she was born, she was the cherished eldest daughter of the marquis’s legitimate line.

Ji Wanqing had loving parents, an older brother who doted on his younger sister, and a younger sister born from the same pregnancy.

The birth of the twin sisters brought boundless joy to this small family. Ji Wanqing enjoyed a happy childhood, growing up happily under the care of her parents and older brother.

Unfortunately, the good times did not last long; their blissful life was shattered overnight. When Ji Wanqing was thirteen, her father and older brother set out for battle, only to be killed in battlefield together.

Her older brother, just eighteen years old, died instantly on the battlefield, while her father was gravely injured. He was brought back to the capital after a difficult journey, but passed away just a few days later.

Her mother, a frail woman, could not bear the sudden loss of her husband and son; she fell ill for half a month and eventually passed away as well.

Their happy little family was shattered in an instant; of the five family members, only Ji Wanqing and her twin sister remained.

Amid their grief, a series of practical problems came crashing down upon them.

The title of Marquis of Jingbei was hereditary. Since their father had died without a son, the title passed to their uncle. With the marquisate under new ownership, although Ji Wanqing and her sister remained the legitimate daughters of the household, their circumstances were no longer what they had been.

Under these circumstances, the sisters were not expected to attract any further attention. After completing the three-year mourning period, their uncle would arrange a marriage for them, and they would simply become wives.

But little did anyone expect that Ji Wanqing would be summoned by the Empress the very moment her mourning period ended.

Speaking of this Empress, she also bore the surname Ji and shared the same ancestral lineage as Ji Wanqing—she was her father’s cousin.

The ancestors of the Ji family were founding heroes, bestowed the title of Marquis of Linjiang by the Founding Emperor, and granted a “Dan Shu Tie Quan” (a document guaranteeing immunity from punishment). The title was hereditary and could never be revoked; by the time it reached Ji Wanqing’s great-grandfather, it had already been passed down through three generations.

Grandfather Ji had several sons, both legitimate and illegitimate. The eldest legitimate son was designated as the heir apparent to inherit the title—this goes without saying—and the new Marquis of Linjiang was none other than Empress Ji’s father.

Grandfather Ji also had a capable illegitimate son who joined the army at a young age and achieved remarkable military achievements. He was conferred the title of Marquis of Jingbei by the previous emperor, a title also hereditary; this was Ji Wanqing’s biological grandfather.

With two marquises in the Ji family, the clan reached the height of its prestige at that time. Both the legitimate and illegitimate sons were capable, and the family’s fortunes continued to prosper.

It wasn’t until the first Marquis of Jingbei passed away, that Ji Wanqing’s father inherited the title, and Empress Ji ascended as the Empress. At that time, Empress Ji, driven by her ambitious aspirations to seize control of the Eastern Palace, held political views that were directly opposed to Ji Wanqing’s father. Thus, the two families gradually drifted apart.

It was the reason that Ji Wanqing, as the eldest legitimate daughter of the Marquis of Jingbei, had not paid her respects to the Empress for many years.

Thinking of this, Ji Wanqing gave a faint, wry smile. Her uncle was a man of mediocre ability who paid close attention to the Empress and the Marquis of Linjiang’s household; he had been quick to curry favor with them, and the two families had long since reconciled.

Ji Wanqing sighed inwardly. Since the title had already been inherited by another, she didn’t care how they chose to act—but she wondered what kind of agreement the two families had reached that had led to her being summoned to the palace today.

Otherwise, as an orphan who had lost both parents, she would never have been summoned to the palace immediately after the end of her mourning period.

“Her Majesty has summoned you. Miss Ji, please follow this servant.”

A palace maid dressed in apricot-colored robes approached. Judging by her attire, she must be a senior maid in the Empress’s service. She took over from the attendant who had been guiding the way and led Ji Wanqing toward the main hall.

Ji Wanqing composed herself and followed quietly, keeping her emotions in check. Where there’s smoke, there’s fire—but no matter what the other party had in mind, they would have to reveal their hand; she would simply wait and see how things unfolded.

“This humble servant pays her respects to Her Majesty the Empress. May Her Majesty live ten thousand years, ten thousand years, ten thousand, ten thousand years.”

Upon entering the main hall, she found herself in a room resplendent with gold and splendor. The palace attendants stood quietly with their heads bowed. Ji Wanqing did not look around; she simply lowered her gaze and performed a deep bow.

A slightly hoarse female voice came from the main seat, and the speaker laughed warmly, “No need for such formalities. Please rise at once.”

Immediately, a senior palace maid came forward to assist Ji Wanqing. As she rose, she cast a brief glance toward the person above her.

The Empress, clad in a bright yellow phoenix robe, sat in the main seat. Though nearing forty, she appeared to be no more than her early thirties. She had a long face, almond-shaped eyes, and radiant skin; her makeup was exquisitely applied. She was smiling warmly, appearing quite approachable and benevolent.

“You silly child, you should address me as ‘Aunt.’ Why are you being so formal? Come here to your aunt.” The Empress spoke with a smile as she beckoned her over.

Ji Wanqing remained unmoved. If she were truly kind, she wouldn’t have made her perform the full kowtow ceremony before helping her up; it was all just a charade.

Though her mind remained clear, Ji Wanqing complied readily. She flashed a cheerful smile and walked forward slowly. “Wanqing obeys your command.”

By addressing herself by her given name rather than as “this humble maiden,” she seemed much more approachable.

Yet, a hint of the timidity one feels upon first meeting a stranger still lingered in Ji Wanqing’s eyes—a trait perfectly matching the image of an orphaned girl in mourning who had not seen outsiders for three years.

The Empress, looking down from her elevated position, scrutinized the young girl before her. Though young, the girl was strikingly beautiful, and whether walking or paying her respects, her demeanor was impeccable. A clear sign of her fine upbringing and the education she had received for over a decade as the daughter of a distinguished family.

At first glance, the Empress was very pleased. For the past few months, she had been searching for a suitable noblewoman, and Ji Wanqing’s background was just right. Now that she had been summoned to the palace, she certainly did not disappoint.

The Crown Prince had reached marriageable age and was due to wed; this was precisely why the Empress had been searching for a noblewoman.

As for why she sought an orphaned girl of questionable background as a wife for the Crown Prince, the reason was simple: the Crown Prince was not Empress Ji’s biological son.

The current Crown Prince was born to Empress Yuan. From a young age, he had been intelligent and studious, possessing both talent and virtue. Upon coming of age, he entered the court to assist the Emperor, achieving remarkable accomplishments and earning a fine reputation both within and outside the court. Moreover, he was gentle and refined, respectful to the wise and humble scholar. He was greatly praised by members of the imperial clan and court officials alike.

Apart from a slight physical weakness caused by a congenital illness, this Crown Prince could almost be described as the “perfect person” in the saying “no one is perfect.”

Faced with such a Crown Prince, Empress Ji felt immense pressure. Having finally become the Empress and given birth to a legitimate son—with the imperial throne just one step away—who could possibly be content?

Empress Ji, in any case, was not willing to accept this. It was no secret that her faction, centered around her and her two sons, had been working tirelessly to seize the throne.

Since ancient times, political marriages have been a key means of consolidating power. If handled skillfully, the position of Crown Princess could give the Eastern Palace a significant boost.

Empress Ji was naturally unhappy. Consequently, she began searching for a suitable candidate—preferably someone who could secure the position of Crown Princess Consort to prevent the Crown Prince from expanding his influence, while also serving as her spy within the Crown Prince’s Palace in case of unforeseen circumstances.

The position of Crown Princess Consort was not easily obtained; after all, not just any woman is qualified to hold it.

Moreover, even if the Crown Princess is not trusted, there were still many things she could accomplish. The Empress hoped to select someone from her own family to serve as her eyes and ears deep within the Eastern Palace.

Killing two birds with one stone.

However, the Empress also had reservations. What if the Crown Princess were to give birth to a legitimate son? In that case, the House of the Marquis of Linjiang or the

House of the Marquis of Jingbei might switch sides—after all, aligning with the Crown Prince would be no more troublesome than supporting her and her son.

This matter required the utmost caution.

After much deliberation, the Empress finally found a suitable candidate from her own family: Ji Wanqing.

Ji Wanqing came from a distinguished family and was the descendant of a meritorious official; she would certainly not bring dishonor to the position of Crown Princess. Moreover, she was an orphan with absolutely no power to support her. Even if she became Crown Princess, it would merely mean one more Crown Princess in the Eastern Palace.

Furthermore, there was another crucial point: the current Marquis of Jingbei was merely her uncle, so the likelihood of him switching sides to support her is extremely slim.

Afterwards, the Empress would arrange for her own son to marry the Marquis of Jingbei’s daughter—she could eliminate any future threats once and for all.

Before summoning Ji Wanqing, the Empress had already thought everything through clearly. She was confident in her plan; if the young woman seemed suitable, she would proceed as planned.

Now, having met her in person, Ji Wanqing’s appearance and demeanor were unexpectedly excellent, which pleased the Empress even more.

She beckoned the young girl closer, took her hand, and smiled, saying, “What a lovely young lady. Your aunt is delighted at first sight. In the future, please come to the palace often to keep your aunt company.”

Ji Wanqing’s brow twitched slightly as a sense of foreboding washed over her, but she kept her composure, smiling cheerfully as she replied, “Wanqing thanks her aunt.”

Out of the corner of her eye, she glanced at their clasped hands. The Empress’s hands were exceptionally well-cared-for; her fingers were encased in ruby-inlaid nail guards. Her hands were warm, but the nail guards were icy cold, and the sharp tips of the nail guards brushed against her skin, causing a slight stinging sensation.

Ji Wanqing’s eyes flickered slightly, but the smile on her face—tinged with a hint of timidity—remained unchanged.

“You are a daughter of the Ji family. Although your path has been full of hardship and great changes, you still have your aunt here.” The Empress grew increasingly pleased as she looked at her, sensing that Ji Wanqing would not be difficult to control. She patted the girl’s hand, her smile deepening.

“Wanqing thanks you, Aunt.”

The Empress ordered a palace attendant to bring a round stool and had Ji Wanqing sit beside her. Smiling warmly, she said, “You’re all grown up now and of marriageable age. I’ll take great care to find you a good family.”

The second bombshell dropped, and Ji Wanqing was startled.
