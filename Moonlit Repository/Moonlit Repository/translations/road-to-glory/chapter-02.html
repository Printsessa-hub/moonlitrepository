ROAD TO GLORY · Chapter 02

Chapter 2

Ji Wanqing was startled for a moment, but then it dawned on her. That’s right—as a powerless orphan, the only assets she had left to leverage were her family name and a marriage alliance.

As her sons grew older, Empress Ji’s ambition to secure the position of Crown Prince for one of them became blatantly obvious—even a young lady confined to her boudoir like Ji Wanqing had heard whispers of it.

Since she had such ambitions, gathering support was essential—and marriage alliances were an excellent means to that end: quick, convenient, and with immediate results.

There were many young ladies in the Marquis of Linjiang’s household; several were slightly older than those in the Marquis of Jingbei’s household. Over the past few years, they had gradually been married off, and every one of their husbands’ families was a household with which Empress Ji sought to strengthen ties.

The remaining ones were still too young to be of any use for the time being—so had they set their sights on her?

However, it was not that Ji Wanqing was being overly self-deprecating; in reality, as an orphaned girl, her value as a marriage prospect was not particularly high.

Ji Wanqing’s thoughts raced, but her face betrayed a look of hesitation as she said, “Aunt, I am deeply grateful for your concern, but when my parents were still alive, it seems they had already arranged a marriage for me.”

Whatever she could think of, the Empress surely already knew; besides, she couldn’t directly say she held little value as a bride. So Ji Wanqing found an excuse to back off, hoping the other party was merely acting on a passing whim and would let the matter drop.

Though that possibility was clearly slim.

“Though my parents have passed away, as their daughter, I absolutely cannot disregard their wishes.” Ji Wanqing held her handkerchief and gently dabbed at the corner of her eye.

“Are you referring to the Wang family of the Dongchuan Marquis’s House?” The Empress smiled and said calmly, “Your two families haven't reached an agreement yet, have you? As far as I know, the Lady of the Wang family began looking for a marriage partner for the Heir Apparent as early as last year.”

Clearly, now that Lady Wang had seen Ji Wanqing had become an orphan, she was no longer willing to proceed with the marriage—the matter had already fallen through.

Ji Wanqing knew full well that this was merely an excuse, yet the Empress’s air of complete certainty proved she had already investigated the matter thoroughly in advance.

From this casual probe alone, it seemed her value as a marriage prospect was far greater than she had ever imagined.

This was clearly illogical, and Ji Wanqing’s sense of crisis intensified. At the same time, she knew this had been planned for a long time, and there was no way for her to avoid it.

Having lived in ancient society for fifteen years, Ji Wanqing was acutely aware of the power of imperial authority. The saying that “when the Son of Heaven grows angry, a million corpses lie in his wake” was no joke at all; though the Empress was not the Son of Heaven, disposing of a young orphaned girl would pose absolutely no difficulty for her.

For now, her only option was to play it by ear. Ji Wanqing seemed to recall a sorrowful memory, and her eyes welled up with tears; she hurriedly wiped them away with a handkerchief. “Aunt, please forgive Wanqing for this breach of etiquette.”

“You silly child—what crime is there in missing your parents?”

The Empress’s smile deepened. Ji Wanqing was even more suitable than she had imagined—clever and pragmatic, able to quickly assess her situation and make concessions. This would make it easier to manipulate her with threats and promises in the future.

She certainly didn’t need a fool who was like “preaching to a bull.” Compared to securing the Crown Prince’s position, the Empress placed greater importance on planting spies deep within the Eastern Palace.

“The Wang family has broken their word; we’re better off without them. But since you are a daughter of the Ji family, how could I not be concerned?” The Empress spoke with a tone of pity as she removed a gold bracelet inlaid with pearls and twisted threads and slipped it onto Ji Wanqing’s wrist, patting it gently.

“Rest assured, I will keep your marriage arrangements in mind.”

The gold bracelet gleamed brightly and felt heavy, yet Ji Wanqing felt only an icy, bone-chilling cold against her skin. Her heart sank, and she heard herself whisper, “Wanqing thanks you, Aunt.”

The Empress was pleased. After exchanging a few more words, she smiled and said, “You may go back now. Another day, I’ll invite you back to the palace for a little gathering.”

The apricot-robed palace maid reappeared, leading Ji Wanqing out of the Kunning Palace. This time, she was treated significantly better, as she was escorted directly by one of the Empress’s senior palace maids.

Ji Wanqing glanced at the apricot-robed figure ahead of her and pursed her lips; this kind of treatment was not what she wanted.

Walking one behind the other, they left Kunning Palace and turned toward the palace gates. As they passed by the Imperial Garden, however, she encountered someone she hadn’t expected.

A group of people emerged from behind a rockery, walking along the bluestone path through the Imperial Garden. The man at the head wore a purple-and-gold crown that held his hair in place and a bright yellow brocaded robe.

The only people who could wear bright yellow so brazenly were the Emperor and the Crown Prince. A five-clawed design signified a dragon, while a four-clawed one signified a python. As Ji Wanqing glanced up, she caught sight of the python pattern on the young man’s brocade robe.

Judging by the man’s age and attire, he was clearly the Crown Prince of the current dynasty.

Ji Wanqing had once heard her late father speak of the Crown Prince, describing him as “virtuous, capable, respectful, and humble, possessing great talent—he will surely bring peace to the realm.”

Her father had always held the Crown Prince in the highest regard, which was why he had gradually distanced himself from Empress Ji and the Marquis of Linjiang’s household. Ji Wanqing vaguely understood her father’s intentions: the current Emperor Changping was not a wise ruler, and her father hoped the Crown Prince would ascend the throne smoothly, purge the court of treacherous officials, and bring greater prosperity to the dynasty.

Alas, he did not live to see that day; he had given his life for the country.

Ji Wanqing’s spirits sank instantly. In that brief moment of distraction, the Crown Prince’s entourage had drawn near. The apricot-robed palace maid at the front had already knelt to pay her respects, so she immediately gathered her composure and bowed along with her.

“No need for such formalities. Rise.” Crown Prince Gao Xu nodded slightly, glancing at the two before him. He recognized the senior palace maid at the Empress’s side at a glance; she left a distinct impression on him.

He could not possibly be unaware of Empress Ji’s schemes, yet Gao Xu’s voice remained gentle, without a trace of coldness. Ji Wanqing caught a glimpse of him out of the corner of her eye; though he possessed a commanding presence, his expression was serene and amiable, showing not the slightest hint of displeasure. Indeed, he lived up to his reputation as “gentle and refined.”

Gao Xu’s gaze swept past Ji Wanqing. The young woman looked unfamiliar to him. He paused briefly before asking, “Are you the daughter of Ji Zongqing, the Marquis of Jingbei?”

He was asking a question he already knew the answer to. The Eastern Palace had its own channels of information, and Gao Xu had long been aware that Empress Ji had summoned the eldest daughter of the late Marquis of Jingbei, who had just completed her mourning period.

As for the Ji clan’s ambitions toward the Eastern Palace, he deeply despised them; yet, among the Ji family, there was one person for whom he harbored a favorable impression.

This was Ji Wanqing’s biological father, Ji Zongqing, the former Marquis of Jingbei.

Aside from Ji Zongqing’s steadfast refusal to support Empress Ji—even at the cost of parting ways with the Marquis of Linjiang’s household. What mattered most was his bravery and military prowess. For decades, he had loyally defended the nation, and in the end, to repel the Tartar invasion, both father and son fell on the battlefield. Truly, they were remarkable heroes, worthy of admiration.

The deaths of Ji Zongqing and his son were a source of deep sorrow and regret, and as Crown Prince, Gao Xu was no exception—which was why he spoke up now.

Upon hearing this, Ji Wanqing felt a lump in her throat and nearly shed a tear. She had not expected that, three years after her father’s passing and long after her uncle had inherited the title, there were still those who referred to her father as the Marquis of Jingbei.

She forced herself to hold back her emotions. “In response to Your Highness the Crown Prince, that is indeed correct.”

Gao Xu nodded. “Your father and elder brother defended the country and protected the people; they are a shining example of loyalty and righteousness in the Great Zhou Dynasty.”

Tears eventually wet her eyelashes, and Ji Wanqing’s heart felt terribly heavy. It was true that her father and brother had defended their homeland and fulfilled their duty to the state, but in doing so, they had abandoned their own family. Her mother had followed them in death, leaving only her and her younger sister to fend for themselves in loneliness.

After her father and brother had given their lives for the country, because she and her younger sister were women, the words of comfort offered by female relatives were nothing more than words of condolences. This was the first time in three years that Ji Wanqing had heard anyone speak so positively of her father and brother.

Such high praise came from the Crown Prince—the very person her father had held in the highest regard during his lifetime.

Ji Wanqing was suddenly overcome with emotion; setting aside all notions of social hierarchy, she abruptly looked up at him. Through a slight mist of tears, she met a pair of deep, dark eyes.

The Crown Prince had eyes that were extremely dark and luminous. At that moment, his expression remained gentle, yet his eyes were tinged with a hint of deep sorrow—sincere and filled with regret.

He did not rebuke Ji Wanqing for her breach of etiquette. After a moment’s gaze, he said, “Miss Ji, please accept my condolences”

At that moment, she likely would have choked up the moment she spoke, so Ji Wanqing said nothing; she merely bowed her head and nodded.

After their brief exchange, Gao Xu said no more. He left the Imperial Garden, boarded his palanquin, and set off toward the Eastern Palace.

No sooner had he returned to the Eastern Palace, a trusted aide reported that Grand Secretary Wu had been waiting for quite some time.

This Grand Secretary Wu was none other than Wu Zhengyong, the Crown Prince’s maternal grandfather and a minister of the Cabinet. Gao Xu immediately made his way to the main hall.

“Your Highness, I have heard that Empress Ji summoned the eldest daughter of the former Marquis of Jingbei.”

Wu Zhengyong had received the news a step too late; last night, the palace gates had been locked, and he could not enter. As soon as court adjourned today, he hurried straight here.

Both anxious and furious, he exclaimed indignantly, “This is utterly preposterous! His Highness the Crown Prince is the heir apparent to the country—how can he, how can he be matched with an orphaned girl?”

His aged face flushed crimson, furious both at Empress Ji’s sordid motives and at the Emperor’s foolishness—fearing the Crown Prince’s competence, the Emperor had allowed the Empress to act with impunity.

While the Crown Prince’s marriage to a consort would certainly help consolidate the power of the Eastern Palace, if all else failed, Wu Zhengyong also hoped his grandson would find a virtuous wife—one who could ensure marital harmony, making the inner palace a place of rest, and stabilize the imperial household.

Wu Zhengyong had previously planned that, if all else failed, he would have his granddaughter marry the Crown Prince. As first cousins, such a union would strengthen family ties; even if it did not increase the Eastern Palace’s influence, the Emperor likely would not object.

Unexpectedly, Empress Ji’s motives were so despicable. Wu Zhengyong furrowed his brow deeply. “How could a daughter of the Ji family possibly become the Crown Princess?”

Gao Xu and his maternal grandfather took their seats as host and guest. After a moment’s pause, he said, “Miss Ji is the daughter of Ji Zongqing.”

He never denied that Ji Zongqing had distinguished himself through his military achievements.

When the topic turned to the late Marquis of Jingbei, Wu Zhengyong remained silent for a long moment. “The late Marquis of Jingbei was upright and loyal—a man of utmost admiration. But this eldest Miss Ji is merely a woman, and having been raised by her uncle and aunt for three years, who can say what she is like now?”

Wu Zhengyong held Ji Zongqing in high regard as well, but traditionally, a daughter’s upbringing fell to her mother. A young lady confined to the inner quarters of the mansion had limited exposure to the world.

Moreover, since Empress Ji had this plan in mind, she would inevitably have someone brainwash Ji Wanqing. A young girl who had lost her father; such a feat would be no difficult task.

Gao Xu remained silent upon hearing this; he fully understood what his maternal grandfather had in mind. After a long pause, he said calmly, “If she behaves herself, she will live out her days in peace. If not, however…”

A cold gleam flashed across his eyes, which were usually gentle and serene.

Wu Zhengyong could only shake his head and sigh inwardly; he didn’t hold much hope for the person Empress Ji had specifically sought out.
