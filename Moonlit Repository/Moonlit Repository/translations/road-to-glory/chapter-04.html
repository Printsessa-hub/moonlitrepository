ROAD TO GLORY · Chapter 04

Chapter 4

These days, young ladies from prominent families usually learn various etiquette and rules, as well as needlework, by the time they are just over ten years old. By the time they are eleven or twelve, they begin looking for a husband; after carefully selecting a suitor for two or three years, they become engaged following their coming-of-age ceremony, and marry a year or two later.

When the Ji sisters’ parents passed away, they were already thirteen years old. Suitable marriage partners had already been identified, and both families had reached a tacit agreement.

As for Ji Wanqing, there is no need to elaborate: the Wang family of the Dongchuan Marquis’s mansion had been long-standing friends of the Ji family. However, following a sudden turn of events, Lady Wang abandoned the idea and set out to find another noblewoman for the heir.

As for Ji Wanxiang, the situation was far more complicated.

Their younger daughter had a gentle and delicate disposition, so the Ji parents gave the matter even more careful consideration. Fortunately, Ji Wanxiang had a childhood friend named Zheng Yi. He was the son of a trusted general under her father’s command. Although his family held no title and was somewhat modest in means, they had a small household and did not observe the cumbersome rules typical of wealthy families.

Their fathers shared a bond forged through life-and-death experiences, and the two children had long nurtured secret feelings for one another. Naturally, they agreed to become engaged and marry in two years’ time.

Unfortunately, a major battle that year proved exceptionally brutal. Not only did Ji Wanqing lose her father and brother, but Zheng’s father also gave his life for the country. The Zheng family, having only just begun to rise, lost its pillar of support and fell into ruin overnight.

After this upheaval, Ji Wanqing specifically instructed her people to keep an eye on the Zheng family. They had not broken their word; Zheng Yi still looked forward to marrying Ji Wanxiang. Unfortunately, one was still the daughter of a marquis, while the other was merely a junior military officer observing mourning at home.

Zheng Yi had clearly inherited his father’s spirit; given time, he might well achieve great success and fame. Most importantly, his devotion to Ji Wanxiang was unwavering, and he had eyes for no one else.

Unfortunately, the two families had not yet formalized their engagement, and now it would be extremely difficult to rekindle their relationship—the Marquis of Jingbei’s household alone stood as a barrier separating the two.

Ji Wanqing had given this matter much thought long ago and planned to make a serious effort after her mourning period to finalize the arrangement, but she never expected that plans would fail to keep up with changing circumstances.

She dismissed the servants from the room, wiped away her younger sister’s tears, and asked gently, “Little sister, tell me, do you still wish to marry Zheng Yi?”

Hearing this long-unspoken name, Ji Wanxiang’s heart skipped a beat. Of course she did. He had protected her, cherished her, and doted on her since childhood. When she first learned that her parents had set their sights on Zheng Yi, she had been overjoyed, her heart set entirely on becoming his bride.

But today was different from the past. Though Ji Wanxiang was delicate, she was no fool. Her second uncle and aunt would absolutely never allow her to marry a low-ranking military officer—especially one who had been observing a three-year mourning period at home.

Tears welled up in her eyes again; she hastily wiped them away and feigned composure. “I haven’t thought about Brother Zheng in a long time.”

As sisters in the same predicament, how could Ji Wanxiang bear to put her elder sister in a difficult position over something she herself was powerless to change? If life at the Duke of Han’s mansion truly became unbearable, then she would simply follow her parents and older brother. When that moment finally came, the only person she could not bring herself to part with would be the sister standing before her.

Ji Wanxiang made up her mind, took a deep breath to suppress the ache in her chest, and forced a smile.

“What nonsense are you talking about, you silly girl?”

After all, the two had been together since their mother’s womb; Ji Wanqing could see right through her younger sister’s thoughts at a glance. She shook her head and said, “As for that Seventh Young Master of the Duke of Han’s household—let whoever wants to marry him go ahead. In any case, I won’t allow you to marry him.”

She snorted coldly. “Since Second Aunt thinks it’s a good idea, let her daughter marry him. If that really doesn’t work out, she can always remarry herself.”

Ji Wanqing took her younger sister’s hand and spoke earnestly, “I’ll take care of this right away. If you still have feelings for Zheng Yi, I’ll use this opportunity to arrange the match. If not, I’ll find someone else for you.”

“No, I don’t want anyone else,” Ji Wanxiang blurted out in a moment of panic.

Ji Wanqing smiled. “Then it’s Zheng Yi.”

She furrowed her brow and pondered for a moment, then immediately summoned her wet nurse, Granny He, into the room. She gave her detailed instructions, telling her to go out at once and inquire about the Zheng family, especially Zheng Yi himself, to see if his feelings had remained unchanged.

Whenever her elder sister gave instructions to the servants, Ji Wanxiang would listen quietly without interrupting, and this time was no exception. Only after watching Granny He’s figure disappear through the door did she say with concern, “Sister, I’m afraid this will be extremely difficult to accomplish.”

Ji Wanxiang certainly hoped to marry Zheng Yi, it was her best option at the moment—but she absolutely could not sacrifice her own sister for it. Though her elder sister was capable, she was ultimately constrained by her circumstances. After much deliberation, Ji Wanxiang felt that this matter could not be resolved peacefully; for a moment, her eyebrows knitted tightly together, and she looked deeply troubled.

“Sister, you must not sacrifice yourself for my sake. I cannot agree to that.” Ji Wanxiang’s face hardened, and her voice took on a rare firmness.

“Little sister, don’t overthink it. Even if I face hardship in the future, it will certainly not be because of this matter.” Ji Wanqing shook her head and sighed softly. “We are sisters from the same womb; if you are in this situation, how could I possibly be spared?”

Ji Wanxiang instantly recalled her sister being summoned to the palace; her heart sank, and she panicked. “Sister, why did Her Majesty the Empress summon you to the palace? She—she…”

“Our father and brother defended our home and country for many years, only to give their lives for it. Why, why…” Why must the imperial court continue to make things so difficult for them?

Ji Wanxiang thought of her loving parents and her kind-hearted older brother, then reflected on their current predicament. How the two sisters were being coerced at every turn—and couldn’t help but feel a wave of sorrow wash over her; she burst into tears.

Upon hearing this, Ji Wanqing’s face darkened with sorrow. After a moment, she gathered her composure and comforted her younger sister, “Don’t panic, little sister. Where there’s a will, there’s a way, we’ll find a solution.”

She pressed her lips together, her expression resolute. “They’ve been plotting this for a long time; I’m afraid this situation is unavoidable. Since that’s the case, let’s just consider it a way to collect some ‘interest’ first.” If they wanted her to comply, they would have to resolve her sister’s situation.

No matter how much the Empress wanted to win people over, she couldn’t possibly lose all sense of dignity. Ji Wanxiang’s situation was clearly the Jingbei Marquis’s household’s scheme; after all, those with nothing to lose have nothing to fear. With no family ties to hold them back, the sisters were in a position to take the plunge.

Thinking of her parents, Ji Wanqing finally broke down in tears. After a while, she forced herself to compose her emotions and ordered Lihua and the others to bring hot water.

She wrung out a handkerchief and wiped Ji Wanxiang’s face. “Little sister, don’t cry. Have you forgotten the promise we made to Father and Mother that we would live well?”

Ji Wanxiang held back her tears as best she could, finally managing to stop them, and nodded vigorously. “Sister, I haven’t forgotten.”

With that settled, Ji Wanqing instructed the maids to help her younger sister return to her room to rest. She sat quietly, waiting for news from Granny He.

Granny He worked swiftly. Moreover, Zheng Yi, who had just emerged from the mourning period, was eagerly awaiting news and was actively trying to gather information about the Jingbei Marquis’s household. As soon as the two met, they quickly exchanged information.

Zheng Yi was the eldest son of his family, with a mother and younger siblings to support; he had to provide for his household as soon as possible. However, unlike the Ji sisters, he was a man and could rely on the support of his father’s former comrades.

Given his father’s rank at the time, the family was entitled to a hereditary military post. Three years ago, they had arranged for the eldest son to join the Imperial Garrison in the capital as a seventh-rank captain, with plans to transfer him to the border army in two years so he could follow in his father’s footsteps and achieve military glory.

Now that Zheng’s father had passed away, his father’s former comrades-in-arms remained. Bonds forged through life-and-death experiences in the military were particularly strong, and they had already contacted Zheng Yi, arranging for him to travel to the border town after the mourning period, as originally planned. The Zheng family had already packed up and was preparing to leave the capital.

Zheng Yi’s path forward was set, but he found it hard to let go of Ji Wanxiang. He knew full well that once he left, they would become strangers, yet he still clung to a sliver of hope that he might one day marry the woman he loved.

When he saw the messenger sent by Ji Wanqing, he was overjoyed and, naturally, agreed to everything without hesitation.

This outcome was excellent. Ji Wanqing was very familiar with the Zheng family; they were all warm-hearted and trustworthy people. It would be better for her younger sister to leave the capital after her marriage than to remain there.

Since everything was in place, Ji Wanqing did not hesitate. It was only the hour of the monkey; it would be better to settle the matter that very day to prevent her second aunt from causing any trouble tomorrow.

She sent someone to find her second aunt. The matron returned to report that the second lady was not there—she had gone to Yanshou Hall.

That was just as well; it spared her the trouble of sending someone to invite her.

Ji Wanqing straightened her robe, stood up, and headed straight for Yanshou Hall.

***

Yanshou Hall

Madam He and her daughter-in-law were talking; she sat on one side of the luohan bed, with a woman in her thirties seated below her.

This was none other than Lady Cao, the current wife of the Marquis of Jingbei. With her oval face, long eyebrows, and delicate eyes, she possessed a certain charm. She wore a heavy set of red-gold, pearl-inlaid headdress, a crimson silk brocade jacket, and a matching skirt—her radiant opulence was overwhelming.

Madam He was actually not very pleased with this daughter-in-law. However, back then, her youngest son had not inherited a title; he was of ordinary appearance and lacked any particular talent. When it came to arranging a marriage, she had to settle for what was available, sifting through the options until she selected the legitimate daughter of a mid-ranking official.

Unexpectedly, after Lady Cao entered the household, she proved to be narrow-minded and noisy, which Madam He particularly disliked. However, when Lady Cao’s fortunes later turned, and she became the wife of a marquis, Madam He had no choice but to grant her an ounce of respect.

Madam He had endured her daughter-in-law’s behavior time and again, but when she saw her rambling on about trivial matters without getting to the point, she grew somewhat annoyed. Her expression turned stern as she said, “You’ve come to see me today—what important matter brings you here?”

Lady Cao choked on her words for a moment, then gave an awkward laugh after a long pause. “Mother, please allow me to explain; I do have an important matter to discuss with you.”

Once she got to the point, Lady Cao’s eyes lit up with excitement. “Mother, my brother and sister-in-law have unfortunately passed away, leaving behind my two poor nieces.” She pulled out a handkerchief and pretended to wipe away nonexistent tears. “As their aunt, I naturally must make extra plans for them.”

Madam He’s brow twitched. She suddenly looked up at her excited daughter-in-law and narrowed her eyes. “Don’t forget about the eldest daughter.”

Her daughter-in-law was greedy; could she possibly have found a backup plan? After all, the imperial family would never tolerate a single daughter being betrothed to two families; one mustn’t even entertain such a thought.

“I certainly remember!”

Lady Cao hastened to deny it. No matter how scheming she might be, she wouldn’t dare to meddle with the imperial family. She wasn’t tired of living. She quickly explained, “I merely showed the second daughter a potential suitor.”

Madam He’s stiffened back relaxed, and she picked up her teacup to take a sip. She glanced at Mrs. Cao. “Which family?”

Lady Cao could hardly contain her joy. “It is none other than the Feng family of the Duke of Han’s household. Lady Feng paid a visit yesterday; it turns out she has set her sights on our second daughter and wishes to marry her to her youngest son.”

“The Duke of Han holds a high and influential position; the Feng family is extremely wealthy; and the Seventh Young Master Feng is the legitimate son of a distinguished lineage. This is truly an excellent match.”Lady Cao’s joy was evident; without pausing for breath, she continued, “For our family to form an alliance with them, it would be a great blessing.”

“Mother…”

Madam He remained silent. Lady Cao pressed on, eager to praise the match further, but before she could speak, she was sharply interrupted.

“If the Feng family is such a fine match and the Seventh Young Master Feng is such a talented and promising young man, then my younger sister—who is merely an orphan who has lost both her parents—is certainly unworthy of him.” The curtain was yanked aside abruptly, and Ji Wanqing strode into the room, gazing coldly at Lady Cao.

“Since the Feng family is such a fine household and Seventh Master Feng is such a talented young man, I, a young orphan who has lost both parents, am certainly not a suitable match.” The curtain was suddenly pulled aside, and Ji Wanqing strode into the room, glaring coldly at Lady Cao.

Lady Cao’s voice was quite loud; Ji Wanqing had only just arrived outside the main hall of Yanshou Hall when she overheard the entire exchange. Burning with rage, she didn’t wait for an announcement—she simply raised her hand, pulled back the curtain, and strode inside.

Hearing the commotion, the mother-in-law and daughter-in-law both looked over. Ji Wanqing glared coldly at Lady Cao and snorted derisively, “Since the Feng family is so wonderful, my younger cousin should be the one to marry into it. Otherwise…”

She eyed Lady Cao up and down, then curled her lips and said, “Otherwise, Second Aunt should marry into it herself—that way, we won’t let our own family’s fortune go to outsiders.”

Lady Cao’s fair face immediately flushed crimson. How could she possibly let her own daughter marry a good-for-nothing wastrel? She hadn’t expected Ji Wanqing—a young lady from a respectable family, would so brazenly take over the conversation, especially since her final remark was clearly meant to be humiliating.

It was one thing to have a veil of modesty to hide behind, but once it was forcefully torn away, Lady Cao felt her face burn.

Convinced that every servant in the room was staring at her, she flew into a rage. Her expression hardened, and she said in a deep, stern voice, “Since ancient times, marriage has been decided by the will of the parents and the words of the matchmaker. Since your parents are no longer here, it is naturally up to me and your second uncle to make the decision. How can a young lady like you interfere?”

“Is that so?”

Ji Wanqing walked to the seat opposite Lady Cao and sat down leisurely. Before coming, she had known this would lead to a confrontation; otherwise, she wouldn’t have taunted her. She had already prepared a countermeasure.

She raised her eyes, glancing at her grandmother, who had remained silent, then at Lady Cao, who was visibly smug. She smiled. “I thought that since my second aunt had made the decision for me, she would be fully satisfied.”

“Hour of the monkey” means late afternoon that typically spans from 3:00 PM to 5:00 PM

Luohan bed is a traditional multi-purpose Chinese daybed or couch used for sitting, relaxing, and receiving guests.
