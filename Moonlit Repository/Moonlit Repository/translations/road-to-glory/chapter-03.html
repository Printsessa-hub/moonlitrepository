ROAD TO GLORY · Chapter 03

Chapter 3

The carriage of the Marquis of Jingbei’s residence had been waiting outside the Xuanwu Gate. As soon as Ji Wanqing stepped out of the palace gates, she saw her personal maid, Lihua, peeking out from the carriage, gazing anxiously toward the palace gates.

　　

“Miss!” Lihua, with her round face and big eyes, was overjoyed to see her mistress. She hurriedly jumped down from the carriage and rushed forward to help her up.

　　

The master and her servant climbed into the carriage. The coachman cracked his thin whip, and with the clatter of hooves, the carriage turned around and headed back the way they had come.

“Lihua, pour me a cup of tea first.” Ji Wanqing had been walking back and forth for over an hour; she was both thirsty and exhausted. She took the teacup, felt that it wasn’t too hot, and drank two cups in a row before her thirst was finally quenched.

　　

Ji Wanqing sat down on the short couch, and Lihua hurried forward to massage her legs. After a moment, she felt a little better and relaxed, leaning back against the bolster.

　　

Though her movements were graceful, the posture was still somewhat languid; had her mother been alive, she certainly would not have permitted it.

　　

In her previous life, Ji Wanqing had been somewhat unconventional, but now that she was living as an ancient person, she had become extremely mindful of her conduct. While she did not necessarily agree with all these rules, she was terrified of bringing shame upon her parents. She had devoted herself to learning and observing them for over a decade, and they had become ingrained in her bones.

　　

This was for the best; at least she had not disappointed her parents’ long-held expectations.

　　

It had been three years since her parents passed away, and she had finally found some peace. Recently, however, the end of her mourning period had stirred up memories of the past, and Ji Wanqing found herself growing increasingly melancholic.

“Miss,” Li Hua asked anxiously as she massaged her mistress’s legs, “I wonder why Her Majesty the Empress has summoned you to the palace?”

With an innate fear of imperial power, Li Hua kept her voice extremely low, glancing around cautiously as she spoke.

　

Ji Wanqing’s lips curled into a faint, cold smile. “Your young mistress is an orphan. The only things she can be used for are her family name and a marriage.”

　　

“Miss, what should we do?” Lihua, the daughter of Ji Wanqing’s wet nurse, had grown up alongside her mistress since childhood and was utterly loyal; upon hearing this, she was overcome with panic.

　　

“What else can we do? We’re at their mercy. We’ll just have to take things as they come.” Any course of action would have to wait until the other side made their move.

When it came to marriage, Ji Wanqing had mentally prepared herself long ago. While there were certainly couples in this world who were devoted solely to one another, like her parents, they were extremely rare. More often than not, men kept multiple wives and concubines, eager to enjoy the pleasures of having multiple spouses.

Ji Wanqing didn’t believe she would be lucky enough to meet a man like her father. Therefore, she had long made plans for both scenarios: if she did encounter the latter type, she would treat marriage as a job. Since she couldn’t seek a better job, she would hold onto this one firmly until the day she retired—or passed away.

She took it in stride; if the man could develop into a suitable partner and they got along harmoniously, life wouldn’t be so unbearable.

Now, the Empress’s schemes have added risk to this “career,” but her overall approach remains unchanged: she’ll do her best at her job and take things as they come.

Li Hua pressed her palms together and murmured, “Even if it’s an arranged marriage, I hope to find a decent family for the young lady.”

Ji Wanqing remained silent. The whole situation reeked of strangeness; it defied common sense, and a respectable family would likely not consider her. After all, her uncle’s legitimate daughter was almost of marriageable age, and her cousin was now the true daughter of the Marquis of Jingbei.

Still, she said nothing. Lihua was utterly loyal, and Ji Wanqing didn’t want her to worry too much, so she merely nodded in agreement.

The carriage rumbled along, and they soon returned to the Marquis of Jingbei’s mansion. The carriage entered through the side gate and stopped in front of the second hanging flower gate, where Ji Wanqing was helped out of the carriage.

With its double eaves and soaring ridges, and deep, secluded courtyards, the scene before her was familiar—a place where she had once filled the air with endless laughter and joy. Alas, though the place remained the same, the people were no longer there. After three years, Ji Wanqing had long since been tempered by life’s hardships; her expression unchanged, she walked toward the inner hall.

In the Yanshou Hall, the main courtyard of the inner residence, lived Ji Wanqing’s paternal grandmother, Madam He. Her father and uncle were both her grandmother’s sons, and even though the Marquis of Jingbei had been replaced, her grandmother continued to reside there securely.

“Wanqing pays her respects to Grandmother.” Ji Wanqing entered the room and bowed deeply, clasping her hands in front of her, before taking her seat.

“No need for such formalities. Please rise and take a seat.”

Madam He was nearing sixty, but thanks to her good care, she looked no more than fifty. Her skin was fair, and her eyes were bright and lively. Her black hair, gathered in a round bun, was streaked with silver; she wore a wide-brimmed, jade-inlaid browband and a royal blue brocade jacket with auspicious patterns. She was impeccably dressed and looked full of vitality; it seemed the shadow cast by her eldest son’s untimely death had long since faded.

Madam He harbored mixed feelings toward her eldest granddaughter, Ji Wanqing. In the past, she had disliked her eldest daughter-in-law for monopolizing her son’s attention, and consequently, she had not been particularly fond of her two granddaughters. Now that the girls had lost both their parents, however, she felt a greater sense of pity toward them.

Yet none of this mattered as much as the future of the household.

Madam He was well aware that her eldest son was capable, while her second son was mediocre. Now that the second son had inherited the title, since he refused to serve in the military, he stood to gain nothing from the influence his father and elder brother had built up over many years in the army. The decline of the Jingbei Marquis’s household was already evident, and a way out must be found as soon as possible.

The second son was aligning himself with the House of the Linjiang Marquis, home of Empress Ji. Madam He knew exactly why Ji Wanqing had been summoned to the palace; she was fully aware of the situation, yet she had tacitly approved it.

On the surface, Ji Wanqing showed no sign of unease and appeared perfectly calm. Yet it was precisely this calmness that made Madam He feel somewhat embarrassed, as if the other woman had already seen right through her thoughts.

Even though Madam He firmly believed that, as a daughter of the Ji family, it was her duty to support the family when necessary, at that moment, she felt extremely uncomfortable.

Madam He cleared her throat twice and said, “You must be tired after spending half a day in the palace. Go back and have lunch, then get some proper rest.”

Ji Wanqing was indeed very calm. She understood clearly that the moment her father passed away, the master of the Marquis’s mansion had changed, and so had the status of her and her sister. Things had changed; even her grandmother was no longer the same person she once was.

It was very realistic—and very normal.

Ji Wanqing complied without hesitation, rising from the rose-patterned chair she hadn’t even had time to warm, and took her leave to return to her room.

Her courtyard was called Chaoxia Courtyard; it was the finest courtyard in the inner quarters aside from the main hall, carefully selected by her parents years ago. After the tragedy three years ago, Ji Wanqing had brought her younger sister to live with her there.

The sisters lived on either side of the main hall. Ji Wanqing went straight to the inner room on the left, washed her hands and face, and changed into a set of casual clothes, but her sister had not yet come in. She asked in surprise, “Where’s the second young lady? Is she feeling unwell?”

Her younger sister, Ji Wanxiang, had taken after their mother and had a delicate constitution; she had been prone to minor illnesses since childhood. After losing their parents and older brother, her condition had somewhat worsened. Out of concern for her, Ji Wanqing had brought her to live with her, and it was only through her meticulous care that she had gradually recovered.

The sisters had always been very close, and now that they depended on one another for survival, Ji Wanxiang had been pacing anxiously ever since learning of the Empress’s summons yesterday, worried about her elder sister. If nothing had delayed her, she would have rushed in long ago.

“Replying to your question, Young Lady, the Second Young Lady is not unwell; she was simply summoned by the Second Madam.”

The speaker was He Shi, Ji Wanqing’s wet nurse. He Shi had been a maid part of Ji Wanqing’s mother’s dowry; later, she became the young mistress’s wet nurse and cared for her meticulously for over a decade. To put it boldly, she treated the young mistress as her own flesh and blood.

The “Second Madam” she referred to was none other than Ji Wanqing’s aunt—now the Lady of the Marquis. Granny He and the others were quite stubborn; in private, they stubbornly refused to change the way they addressed her.

Granny He was deeply concerned. “The Second Young Lady went to pay her respects to the Grand Madam, but she has not returned even after a long time. I sent someone to inquire and was told that after the gathering at Yanshou Hall dispersed, the Second Young Lady was taken back by the Second Madam.”

Ji Wanqing furrowed her brow. Her aunt came from a modest background and lacked a broad perspective; now that she had risen to prominence, she was full of herself and habitually adopted the haughty demeanor befitting as the Lady of Marquis. Yet summoning the sisters alone to their courtyard for a private conversation was something she had never done before.

It seemed that as soon as the sisters emerged from mourning, all manner of troublesome people immediately began to surface.

Ji Wanqing glanced at the sky; it was already noon. Just as she was about to go out to fetch her younger sister, Ji Wanxiang returned.

The two sisters were twins born of the same pregnancy. As children, they looked extremely alike, but as they grew older, their differences became apparent. Overall, they now bore about five or six parts of a resemblance to one another, both being beauties of exceptional grace.

Ji Wanxiang, however, took more after her mother: her slender, willow-like eyebrows curved gently, and her eyes held a gentle, delicate expression, making her resemble a graceful beauty that stepped out of a classical painting; whereas Ji Wanqing’s eyebrows and eyes took after her father—the outer corners of her eyebrows and eyes were slightly upturned, her expression lively and radiant, adding a touch of vibrant vigor.

Ji Wanqing had a keen eye; even though her younger sister had gone to great lengths to conceal it, she still noticed the faint redness at the corners of her eyes.

Ji Wanxiang had been crying.

“Little sister, what’s going on?” Ji Wanqing hurried forward, took her sister’s hand, and her expression darkened. “Did Second Aunt say something?”

“No,” Ji Wanxiang looked up and saw the lingering fatigue on her sister’s face. A pang of sadness welled up in her heart, and she quickly said, “I just saw how happy Second Aunt and her daughter were together, and it brought back painful memories.”

Ji Wanxiang knew full well how difficult their situation was; she didn’t want to put her older sister, whom she relied on, in a difficult position, so she kept it to herself. After all, there was nothing her sister could do about it, and if she could keep it hidden for a while, she would.

However, Ji Wanqing knew her younger sister very well. While it was true that the sisters had been easily moved to sadness by such sights during the first year, things had gradually improved since then—it was clear that Ji Wanxiang wasn’t telling the whole truth.

“Little sister, is there something you can’t tell me?” Ji Wanqing glanced at the maids and matrons who had accompanied her sister out and said, “If you won’t tell me, I’ll ask them—one of them is bound to speak up.”

Ji Wanqing held the indentures of every servant in Chaoxia Courtyard. After all, as the matriarch for many years, their mother was not ignorant to the ways of the world. She knew full well how difficult life would be for her beloved daughters in the future. On her deathbed, she carefully selected her trusted confidants and entrusted the indentures to her eldest daughter.

Due to her temperament, Ji Wanxiang was not capable of major responsibilities. The people of the Chaoxia Courtyard, and all those left behind by their parents all follow Ji Wanqing’s lead. If she were to ask, she would certainly get an answer.

Ji Wanxiang had no choice but to tell the truth: it turned out that her second aunt had shown her a marriage proposal. After “consulting” her niece’s opinion, the aunt had said the match would be finalized in the near future. Naturally sensitive, she knew her second aunt harbored ill intentions. Unable to find a way to refuse, she silently shed tears.

The inner chamber was deathly silent; the very situation she had long feared had come to pass. Since ancient times, marriage has been a matter decided by parental arrangements and the words of matchmakers; no daughter has ever arranged her own marriage. Since Ji Wanqing and her sister’s parents had passed away, the authority to arrange their marriages fell to their uncle and aunt, who were now the heads of the household.

The two sisters were the orphaned daughters of the former Marquis. After their uncle inherited his elder brother’s title, he was, by all rights, supposed to find suitable husbands for his nieces and provide them with generous dowries to ensure they were well-provided for—only then would they avoid becoming the subject of gossip. Yet there was no shortage of families in the capital who were all smiles on the outside but harbored ill intentions beneath the surface.

Even if their uncle and aunt were to throw their dignity and force the sisters into a pit of fire, the worst that could happen would be public criticism; it would still be feasible. Based on Ji Wanqing’s understanding of her second uncle and his wife, as long as there was sufficient gain to be had, they would certainly be capable of such a thing.

Her expression darkened as she asked, “Which family is Second Aunt referring to?”

Bringing up this deeply distressing matter, Ji Wanxiang could not help but burst into tears once more. She said, “Second Aunt said it was the Seventh Master of the Duke of Han.”

As soon as her younger sister spoke, Ji Wanqing’s expression darkened completely.

The Duke of Han held a high position and great influence; he was precisely the kind of figure Empress Ji’s faction sought to court. The Seventh Young Master of his household was the Duke of Han’s youngest legitimate son. By all means, finding a suitable match for the Seventh Young Master would be a simple matter—it certainly wouldn’t fall to an orphaned girl like Ji Wanxiang.

Unfortunately, the Seventh Young Master had an eccentric nature. Having been born to his parents in their middle age, he had been spoiled rotten. He spent his days causing trouble and neglecting his duties. Even before he was betrothed, he had already bedded every maids in the household—labelling him a notorious playboy in the capital. As a result, no family that cherished their daughter or valued their reputation would even consider him.

Lady Han was deeply anxious. Her son’s temperament was beyond redemption, and as his mother, she was unwilling to see her youngest son settle for a girl from a humble family. After scouring the capital, she finally set her sights on Ji Wanxiang.

Although Ji Wanxiang had lost her father, she was the legitimate daughter of a distinguished family, and her upbringing was far superior than ordinary young ladies.

Ji Wanqing gritted her teeth in fury, raised her hand, and slammed it down on the bedside table with a loud “bang,” exclaiming angrily, “Very well! My dear Second Aunt, how could she be so shameless!”

In fact, aside from the flaws that were common knowledge throughout the capital, Ji Wanqing was also aware of another issue concerning the Seventh Master: he had no qualms about eating meat or vegetables and harbored a fondness for men. However, he knew this was wrong, so he had always concealed it well, and few people were aware of it.

Ever since her father’s death, Ji Wanqing had shouldered the responsibility. She deeply understood the importance of being well-informed, so she instructed her confidants to mingle among the common folk, gathering all sorts of information and secretly passing it on to her every few days.

Although she was largely unable to obtain information concerning the capital’s upper circles, this allowed her to stay as well-informed as possible, lest she be kept in the palace and rendered useless.

Ji Wanqing had several trusted confidants left behind by her father; these people were quite capable, and it was through them that she had accidentally learned about the Seventh Master.

Originally, she would have let it go, but now, her second aunt was actually planning to betroth this repulsive man to her younger sister.

Ji Wanqing was furious; this absolutely could not happen.

She glanced at her younger sister, who was quietly shedding tears, suppressed her anger, and soothed her in a gentle voice: “Don’t worry, little sister. As long as I’m here, this will never happen.”
