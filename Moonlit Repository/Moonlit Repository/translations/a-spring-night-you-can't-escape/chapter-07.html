A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 07

Dry Up

At noon two days later, Shu Cheng was in a meeting with the PR team when her phone began to buzz on the table.

The caller ID showed an unfamiliar local number. Thinking it might be a delivery for the pet probiotics she’d ordered, she ignored it.

Perhaps because the weather had turned colder, Tuantuan had lost her appetite over the past couple of days and was suffering from mild diarrhea. She’d specifically consulted the receptionist, who also owned a cat, and ordered the same brand of probiotics.

Unexpectedly, ten minutes later, the phone rang persistently twice more. After a while, Shu Cheng called back, only to find Secretary Zhong on the other end: “Madam, are you free at 3:00 p.m. today? President He would like you to come to the company headquarters.”

She was taken aback. What could possibly require a special trip to the office? “Did he say what it was about?”

“President He didn’t say anything.”

　 ……

Shu Cheng hesitated for a moment, but agreed anyway.

Secretary Zhong: “Then I’ll pick you up at 2:30.”

“No need to trouble yourself, I’ll come on my own.” She asked, “Do you have access cards at headquarters? How do I get up to see him?”

The memory of being turned away at the entrance of the residential area was still fresh in her mind.

He’s such a busy man—maybe I should carry our marriage certificate with me just in case?

Secretary Zhong was at a loss for words for a few seconds, but quickly regained her composure: “You’re Mr. He’s wife, why would you need an access card? I’ll wait for you in the lobby.”

After hanging up, a text message quickly popped up notifying her that a package had arrived at her apartment door.

Her studio was nowhere near as wealthy as Yunshang, which could afford an entire building in the city center. Driving there from here, including city traffic and traffic lights, would take about forty minutes.

To avoid being late like last time, Shu Cheng left the office an hour early.

The drive went smoothly, and she arrived at Yunshang just after 2:30. She stopped at a nearby café for an iced latte to kill time until exactly 3:00 before heading over.

The Yunshang Tower stands tall among the skyscrapers of the CBD; the autumn sunlight glints off its soaring glass curtain walls, creating a magnificent and dazzling sight.

Stepping into the lobby, several LCD screens were all displaying the same news story: The day before yesterday, Yunshang Group successfully bid 10 billion for the development rights to the commercial complex on the city’s Binjiang A3 plot.

Shu Cheng was stunned. Even as an outsider, she knew that prime location in Binjiang was a coveted prize for countless developers—she never expected Yunshang to actually acquire it.

He Jingting had been traveling frequently for business lately; he must have been busy with this project. It seemed only a workaholic could handle the role of CEO—even when he was feeling so unwell before, Shu Cheng hadn’t seen him take even half a day off.

White-collared professionals in suits and ties came and went in a hurry, while she was the only one looking left and right, making her stand out like a leisurely person.

“Madam, this way, please.” Secretary Zhong led her straight to the innermost area and took the private elevator directly to the top floor.

The view from the 45th floor was spectacular—it felt as if they were perched above the blue sky. Through the floor-to-ceiling windows, the entire CBD business district lay spread out before them.

The office door stood open, and faint voices could be heard from inside.

He Jingting was seated at his sandalwood desk, talking with a middle-aged executive. His expression was serious and focused; the cuffs of his black shirt were rolled up to his elbows, and he idly twirled a pen between his fingertips. His aura, while casual, exuded an air of authority that left no room for the slightest violation.

Catching a glimpse of the figure approaching the door out of the corner of his eye, he briefly concluded his conversation: “Come in.”

This was Shu Cheng’s first time meeting him at work; she walked over and sat down, feeling slightly self-conscious.

Secretary Zhong brought in two fresh cups of tea, closed the door, and withdrew, leaving her alone, feeling completely bewildered.

He Jingting calmly took a sip of tea: “Is there something you’d like to tell me?”

“Me?” For a split second, Shu Cheng’s mind went blank. Hadn’t he specifically called her over to discuss something? Why was he asking her instead?

“Hmm.” He Jingting looked up. “Or perhaps, you have a question.”

At those words, her heart tightened slightly. The matter with the kitten… Had he sensed something? Or could it be something else?

But under the scrutinizing gaze of the man across from her, Shu Cheng clenched the hem of her clothes lightly, opened her mouth, then hesitated.

He Jingting seemed dissatisfied with her reaction; he set down his bone china teacup and swept a cold glance over her way.

Suddenly, there was a soft knock on the office door, and Secretary Zhong ushered someone in: “This is Attorney Zhao.”

The seemingly random conversation came to an end right then.

Attorney Zhao respectfully handed a black folder to Shu Cheng. He Jingting, meanwhile, leaned back casually, calmly twirling his pen as if he were already thoroughly familiar with its contents.

Holding the folder—which was about several dozen pages thick……

“What’s this?” Shu Cheng couldn’t help but break out in a cold sweat. A contract selling her into servitude? Or something else?

She held her breath as she opened it. The heading on the first page actually read: “Marital Property Gift Agreement.”

The text was densely packed in black ink on white paper. Shu Cheng flipped through several pages in confusion but couldn’t find the key points.

Attorney Zhao immediately stepped forward to help turn the pages and thoughtfully summarized and explained: “Mrs. He, to put it simply, Mr. He will conditionally gift you a villa in Shanshui Manor, Yunqi District, Nanshi City, and 20% of the future dividends from 'Yunshang Binjiang Tiandi' through property transfer and trust.”

Villas and dividends.

“Why is he giving me these?” Shu Cheng blurted out the question in her astonishment.

“This is what you deserve for marrying me,” He Jingting said casually, as if these weren’t assets worth hundreds of millions, but simply a set of gold jewelry he was giving away.

He pulled a thin black card from the inside pocket of his suit: “Also, from now on, all your expenses will be charged to this supplementary card.”

She didn’t take it. His hand hovered for a few seconds before he set it directly on the table and pushed it toward her.

Attorney Zhao added, “This was originally intended to be a prenuptial agreement, but since the bidding for ‘Binjiang Tiandi’ wasn’t finalized in time, it was changed to a postnuptial gift.”

Binjiang Tiandi—that plot of land Yunshang had just won at auction, where a shopping mall was about to be built.

“I can’t accept this. You’ve already given me what you owed me.” With Attorney Zhao present, Shu Cheng couldn’t say it outright.

Through his discreet, substantial investments in the Shu family, as well as the integration and sharing of top-tier teams, channels, and supply chains, he had already fulfilled his obligations in this marriage—one with a clearly defined price tag.

“That was a deal between Yunshang and the Shu family.”

He Jingting stood up and walked to the French windows, slowly turning his wristwatch. The sunlight, like molten amber, flowed over his broad shoulders, casting a long, slanted shadow.

“These are my personal guarantees for your marriage into the He family.”

Real estate, ongoing dividends, and disposable cash—he believed he had covered every aspect of her security.

“What do you think is still missing?

Shu Cheng was dazed: “Security?”

She couldn’t help but recall her mother’s situation at home before she passed away. As a legally wedded wife, she had been forced to swallow her anger and put up with Li Lan, tolerating the string of women surrounding Shu Lin…

Could these be the “disclaimers” he’d bought his way out of in advance?

But the man didn’t give her a chance to ask further: “Don’t worry, there are conditions.”

Attorney Zhao took the agreement, flipped to the last page, and handed it to Shu Cheng. The very first clause read: [Party B pledges to strictly observe the duty of marital fidelity during the duration of the marriage and shall not engage in improper sexual relations or emotional entanglements with any third party.]

Shu Cheng carefully read through the dozen or so lines of conditions for the gift.

In simple terms, she was prohibited from engaging in any behavior, whether marital or moral, that would attract public scrutiny or damage the reputation of the group or his personal reputation.

She was also required to fulfill the obligations befitting her status, maintaining the image of a loving couple with him in necessary business and public settings; otherwise, she would face massive compensation claims.

“Once you’re done reading, just sign it.” He Jingting sat back lazily and personally handed her a fountain pen.

Shu Cheng hesitated to take it, biting her lip in silence. Maintaining the image of a loving couple and safeguarding the Group’s interests—these were indeed things he needed.

But while she felt both flattered and overwhelmed, something still felt off; after all, she had never intended to neglect these obligations in the first place.

Since when had marital fidelity become something with a price tag?

The young woman kept her head bowed, her gaze fixed vacantly on the page. Her long hair slid down from her shoulders, falling beside her ear and partially obscuring her expression.

From He Jingting’s perspective, all he could see were her lowered eyelashes and her fingertips, which had been holding the corner of the agreement for a long time, seemingly torn by indecision.

Had giving her a gift actually become a burden? “Afraid I’ll sell you off?”

He Jingting’s expression darkened. He set the pen he was holding down on the wooden table with a soft yet menacing sound, as if delivering a warning tap to Shu Cheng’s head.

“Or is it that you have some other concern—are you afraid you can’t afford the compensation?” His voice rose slightly at the end, like a knife’s edge slicing through the frozen air.

“No.” Shu Cheng shook her head. She had no intention of having an affair, nor did she have the guts to do so.

“Fine,” He Jingting sneered. “Then I’ll send this agreement to your father. I’m sure he’ll be more than happy to sign it for you.”

Shu Cheng froze for a moment, her cheeks flushing slightly with embarrassment.

Her father’s obsequious manner and the nominal nature of their family relationship were things the onlookers had long been well aware of, but now that it had been laid bare so directly, she felt as if she had nowhere to hide her shame.

“No…” She blinked her dry eyes, curled her fingers slightly, and reached for the pen.

It was a rash remark, but there was no taking it back now. He Jingting’s gaze darkened; his thin lips parted slightly in regret before finally pressing tightly together into a thin line.

The atmosphere plummeted, and the sunlight filling the room seemed to wither away.

Suddenly, Secretary Zhong knocked from outside: “Mr. He, someone from HC Medical in Germany is trying to reach you.”

He Jingting stood up, his tone slightly stiff: “I’ll be right back. If you have any questions, see Attorney Zhao.”

Shu Cheng kept her eyes downcast and nodded, not daring to look up at him.

A dozen seconds later, the office door closed again, leaving only the hum of the central air conditioning.

After a while, Shu Cheng’s tense body finally relaxed a little, and her attention returned to the thick agreement. She skimmed through it briefly.

The contents were actually very clear, and the terms were drafted to protect her interests as much as possible. As long as she didn’t make any mistakes, she would be the absolute beneficiary in terms of assets.

So what if this was his “disclaimer”? Given her current situation, she had no right to refuse.

Glaring sunlight streamed in from the top floor; perhaps she was sitting at the wrong angle, as her eyes began to sting slightly. Shu Cheng uncapped the pen and signed her full name in the signature line.

“Mrs. He, I’ll be in touch regarding the next steps,” said Attorney Zhao with a smile as he took the folder and left his business card.

Now she was truly alone.

This office, spanning over 100 square meters, was spotless. Through the floor-to-ceiling windows, the city skyline stretched out in a wide, open view. But aside from the desk and the reception area, it was so minimalist it felt somewhat empty—much like He Jingting himself, devoid of any sense of warmth or life.

Shu Cheng didn’t dare wander around, so she idly spun her chair, feeling a bit bored.

After a while, Secretary Zhong pushed open the door, accompanied by a small cart laden with afternoon tea: “Our restaurant’s afternoon tea is quite good. Please give it a try.”

“Can I leave now?”

“President He is in an emergency meeting. Please wait just a little longer, Madam.”

She looked puzzled: “I’ve already signed the agreement. What else could he possibly have to do?”

“President He didn’t specify that,” Secretary Zhong smiled. “Would you like some herbal tea or coffee?”

Shu Cheng wasn’t in the mood for tea: “Thanks, but I don’t need anything. I’m not hungry yet.”

Secretary Zhong pushed the cart back out exactly as it was. The lid on the dessert hadn’t been lifted, so she didn’t realize it was a slice of her favorite lemon mousse cake—the same kind she’d seen at the bridal shop.

Although she didn’t know what else He Jingting wanted from her, she couldn’t leave now anyway.

Shu Cheng unlocked her phone and tapped on the security feed from her apartment to check on her kitten. Before moving to Yujiang Mansion, she’d installed cameras in every room of her apartment so she could see Tuantuan anytime, anywhere.

She’d go over in a bit to feed him some probiotics—she’d mix them into the newly bought canned salmon; he’d surely love it.

Shu Cheng rotated the camera around the living room and soon spotted Tuantuan curled up by the bed in the bedroom, seemingly taking an afternoon nap. Her heart suddenly melted, and she quickly switched the camera to view the bedroom.

However, the moment she saw the image clearly, Shu Cheng felt as if she’d been plunged into an ice cave—

The kitten was sprawled on the floor, its eyes tightly shut, its tiny body twitching slightly. In front of it lay a large pool of vomit, faintly mixed with undigested food and a dark red tint.

She froze for a few seconds, then rushed out of the office while dialing the property management office.

“Excuse me, Madam. Mr. He hasn’t finished his meeting yet. Please stay inside the office…” Secretary Zhong stood up to stop her, but noticing her panicked expression, asked, “What happened?”

Shu Cheng asked anxiously, “A clinic—can you find a veterinary clinic with an ambulance?”

It was at least a forty-minute drive from here to the apartment; she simply couldn’t wait to get back and take the kitten to the vet!

　　*

That night, the hallway outside the operating room was chaotic, crowded with makeshift IV stands as far as the eye could see. The emergency room door opened and closed repeatedly, and the barking of small dogs rose and fell, mixed with the hushed soothing voices of their owners.

Though old and modest in size, this was the closest large hospital to the apartment complex, equipped with 24-hour emergency care and surgical facilities.

Fortunately, the property management unlocked the door, and the ambulance arrived in time…

Secretary Zhong was scheduled to go on a business trip, but she responsibly accompanied the kitten all the way to the hospital. Only after the kitten was taken into the operating room did she hurriedly hail a cab to catch her flight.

The surgery had just begun half an hour ago, and it was expected to take another hour or so to finish.

Shu Cheng felt incredibly guilty. If she hadn’t spent so little time with Tuantuan lately, he wouldn’t have accidentally swallowed the plastic clasp from his toy. She had naively assumed it was just the cold weather and the change of seasons.

Not far away, a young girl was cradling a tiny stray kitten, no bigger than the palm of her hand. Ignoring its filthy fur, she kept stroking it: “You’ll feel better as soon as the doctor sees you…”

Shu Cheng stared absently at the silhouettes of the young girl and the tiny kitten—two years ago, when Tuantuan was this size, he had come into her life.

Back then, among that litter of stray kittens, only this one with blue eyes was left unclaimed. With white fur and blue eyes, beneath his beautiful appearance lay an inescapable condition: congenital deafness.

Kittens rely on keen hearing to survive, and because of this, Tuantuan had been bullied relentlessly since a young kitten and felt extremely insecure; the moment someone came within a few inches, it would hiss and bar its teeth and claws.

“Never mind. This cat has been returned twice,” the staff member shook his head, leading her into the rescue center. “Why don’t you take a look at this litter of kittens instead? They were just born not long ago, and they’ll be easy to bond with.”

The poor kitten was huddled in the corner of a cardboard box, hissing and trembling timidly. Because it was so frail, it could only protect itself by putting on a brave front.

“It’s okay, I really like it.”

Despite the staff member’s repeated attempts to dissuade her, Shu Cheng resolutely took the kitten home and named it Tuantuan.

At first, she constantly had cuts on her hands, but she cared for it for two whole years—from being skin and bones to developing plump cheeks and a coat that gleamed with a healthy sheen.

The kitten was gradually nourished by love, yet it recognized only Shu Cheng; no one else was allowed to approach it.

The operating room lights remained on the entire time. At one point, a vet nurse came out: “There’s a small section of necrotic intestine. To save its life, we’ll have to remove it. If the owner agrees, please sign here.”

After the nurse left, Shu Cheng buried her face deeply in her hands, her shoulders trembling slightly.

Because of the resection, the surgery took longer than expected. She was trapped in a long, agonizing wait, pacing anxiously at first, then eventually curling up exhausted in a corner, numb with despair.

The emergency room was located on the first floor of the hospital building. The automatic doors at the end of the hallway opened and closed as people came and went, letting in the chilly autumn wind that carried away the last traces of warmth from her body.

Suddenly, a tall, upright figure caught her eye.

Amid the chaos of the emergency room corridor, He Jingting stood out like a sore thumb in his dark gray suit, topped with a long, black trench coat. A light blue surgical mask covered his mouth and nose, revealing only a pair of deep, stern eyes above his high, straight nose.

His sudden appearance seemed to silence all the commotion in an instant. His composed gaze slowly swept across the lobby, pausing briefly at the operating room door before he strode straight toward her.

Shu Cheng looked up as he approached, completely stunned.

It was He Jingting. Was she dreaming?
