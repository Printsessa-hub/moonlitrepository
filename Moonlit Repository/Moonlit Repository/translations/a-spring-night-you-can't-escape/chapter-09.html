A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 09

Severe Pain

For the next few days, He Jingting was nowhere to be seen.

It was only after Shu Cheng searched his name on her browser that she found news reports about him attending a business summit in Gangcheng.

She skimmed through several articles, but none of them mentioned how long the summit was supposed to last.

She closed her phone, suddenly finding the whole situation rather amusing.

They were husband and wife in name and by law, yet they had become so estranged that she had to rely on the news to find out where he was and what he was doing.

Thursday was Lidong, the beginning of winter according to the traditional Chinese calendar. After lunch, Shu Cheng drove to the sanatorium as usual.

The sanatorium was located halfway up a mountain on the western outskirts of the city. The air was fresh, the scenery pleasant, and more importantly, it housed one of the country's top cardiothoracic surgery teams.

Afternoon sunlight streamed into the hospital room, making it warm and dry.

Just as she had when she was a child, Shu Cheng rested her head on Zhou Xiuzhi's lap and quietly breathed in.

There was always a faint scent of traditional Chinese medicine lingering on her grandmother, a familiar fragrance that wrapped around her like the feeling of home.

Zhou Xiuzhi gently stroked her granddaughter's long, black hair. Nothing escaped the old woman's keen eyes.

"Did something happen? Is there something making you unhappy?"

"No..." Shu Cheng gave a soft hum. "I just missed you."

With her weathered fingers, Zhou Xiuzhi slowly peeled an orange. She didn't press the matter. Instead, she patiently removed every bitter strand of white pith before bringing a slice to her granddaughter's lips.

The grandmother and granddaughter chatted and laughed about this and that, and the peaceful afternoon passed in the blink of an eye.

By evening, Shu Cheng stayed for dinner and accompanied her grandmother in eating dumplings.

The setting sun cast a warm glow over everything. From somewhere down the corridor came the distant sound of relatives and medical staff chatting and laughing, while the fragrant aroma of freshly cooked dumplings filled the room.

The handmade dumplings were plump and round, each one stuffed with shrimp and shaped like tiny golden ingots.

Shu Cheng picked one up and popped it into her mouth. Suddenly, she bit into something soft and sweet. It was a red date.

"If you happen to get this dumpling, it means the days ahead will be sweet, and everything will go your way," Zhou Xiuzhi said kindly. "All the unhappy things will pass."

Only then did Shu Cheng realize what her grandmother had been doing when she leaned closer while distributing the dumplings.

She smiled too, warmth spreading through her heart. "Grandma, you too. We'll both be fine."

And yet, in the midst of such a beautiful moment, He Jingting's words from that day inexplicably surfaced in her mind.

—Then when are you planning to tell it?

—Was it because you got married so suddenly, or because you married me?

Now that she thought back on it, beneath those sharp words there had been more than displeasure and mockery. There had also seemed to be a trace of disappointment.

Shu Cheng lowered her eyes, a strange and indescribable feeling stirring in her heart.

Tentatively, she asked, "Grandma, do you remember that when I was little, there was a boy from the He family who lived with us for a while?"

"That eldest son of the He family?" Zhou Xiuzhi's chopsticks paused. "Why are you suddenly asking about him?"

"It's nothing." Shu Cheng took a bite of her dumpling and tried to sound casual. "I ran into him again recently... It was just something work-related. A collaboration involving a brand."

Outside the window, dusk had settled over the city, and a flock of birds flew across the distant sky on their way home.

"That child..." Zhou Xiuzhi gazed out the window and sighed softly.

Seeing that her grandmother didn't seem displeased by the subject, Shu Cheng continued asking, "Do you know why he lived with our family?"

Back then, He Jingting had still been a boy. His father's attitude toward him had always been strange. On the surface, he was polite and affectionate, showing the boy considerable concern.

Yet he had arranged for him to stay in the very last room on the third floor of the old residence—the room that was unbearably hot in summer and freezing cold in winter.

At the time, Shu Cheng had assumed it was because He Jingting was an illegitimate child whose existence had to be kept out of sight.

But as she grew older, she had increasingly felt that something wasn't right. No matter what, he was still a member of the He family of Nanshi.

"His mother was a university student from the mountains who had earned her place through the college entrance examinations. There weren't many like her in those days. I met her once. She was a remarkable girl, very bright and full of spirit..." Zhou Xiuzhi spoke slowly.

"After giving birth to him, she became the subject of some very ugly rumors at school. When the child was only a few months old, she left him at the He family's doorstep and disappeared. She dropped out of university and went north to find work."

The untouched dumplings gradually grew cold.

"That child wasn't very healthy, if I remember correctly. It was the middle of winter, bitterly cold, and he was only a few months old when they left him outside for an entire day and night." Zhou Xiuzhi sighed.

"The He family didn't want to acknowledge him at first. But the police got involved, and the matter ended up in the newspapers. They had no choice but to take him in."

She paused before continuing quietly, "Later, when they sent him to our family... perhaps they were hoping he'd die of illness somewhere outside their home, and they could simply wash their hands of him."

Shu Cheng froze. So that was what had happened...

Even though she herself had never been cherished by her family growing up, she couldn't imagine what it must feel like to know that even the people closest to you were hoping you would die sooner rather than later.

She asked, "Then... they still couldn't find his mother?"

Zhou Xiuzhi gently set down her bowl. "She died a long time ago."

"Because of an illness?"

"They said it was an accident..." A faint sadness appeared in Zhou Xiuzhi's eyes. Perhaps the story had touched an old wound—the pain of having lost her own daughter too young, leaving behind a child.

The elderly woman clearly didn't want to continue talking about it. Instead, she reached for her granddaughter's hand and held it, her voice carrying a profound meaning.

"Chengcheng, all Grandma wishes for is for you to be healthy and happy. Stay away from that world of fame and fortune if you can... In their eyes, feelings mean nothing. Even a person's life isn't worth mentioning."

Shu Cheng looked into her grandmother's aged eyes, feeling the rough pads of her fingers gently brush across her palm.

Her heart grew unbearably heavy. But she had already married He Jingting.

For the rest of her life, she was destined to be entangled in this decadent, glamorous world.

Would he be that heartless too?

****

At 1 a.m., 18th floor of The Peninsula Hotel in Gangcheng

The main lights in the room had deliberately been turned off, leaving only the dim glow spilling in from the corridor of the suite.

He Jingting sat fully dressed on the sofa, his eyes tightly shut. One hand covered his mouth and nose as he breathed heavily and slowly. The position seemed difficult to endure. After leaning there for only a short while, he shifted restlessly and bent forward, his chest heaving even more violently.

The numbers on the pulse oximeter fluctuated continuously. Chen Yanqing's expression darkened.

"How did it suddenly get this bad? Did he come into contact with an allergen today?"

"No." Secretary Zhong lowered his voice. "President He was already feeling unwell on the flight here. He took his medication twice."

"You've been sick for three days, and only now did you think to call me?"

After counting the days, Chen Yanqing was exasperated with him, but he nevertheless quickly reassessed He Jingting's condition and administered another medication through an IV.

Just then, there were two polite knocks at the door. Secretary Zhong went to check. An assistant had brought something over.

Chen Yanqing waved his hand. "Whatever it is, work can wait until tomorrow."

He Jingting's lips moved, but only a faint breath escaped.

Chen Yanqing couldn't make out what he said. He only saw Secretary Zhong close the door and bring over a red paper bag trimmed with gold.

The packaging was old-fashioned yet exquisitely made. In the center were the elegantly written characters “Decheng.”

"President He, we managed to buy everything you asked for."

Curious, Chen Yanqing opened the bag. Inside were a tin of egg rolls, two boxes of butterfly cookies, and egg tarts.

He knew He Jingting never ate sweets. Whenever they had afternoon tea, he wouldn't touch anything except his coffee.

"Yesterday, President Li of Ruiheng mentioned over dinner that his daughter loves the pastries from this place. Whenever he comes to Gangcheng, he always buys some to take home," Secretary Zhong explained. "So, President He had his assistant buy some of their signature items today. The line was incredibly long. It took at least four hours."

He Jingting had barely glanced at the contents before his brows drew together slightly.

"Bring them here..." His words came out with difficulty, his breathing growing heavier.

"All right, stop talking. Get some rest." Chen Yanqing clicked his tongue and quickly placed the bag safely back on the coffee table.

Someone he cared enough about to go through all this trouble for, even while he was sick… It was probably meant for the person waiting for him at home.

He Jingting refused to let the hotel staff be alerted. The IV bag was simply hung from the coat rack, and as the clear medication slowly flowed into his veins, his tightly clenched fingers gradually relaxed.

The night grew deeper. The others retreated to the guest bedroom of the suite, leaving him a quiet space to rest.

During an attack, even lying down to rest became a luxury. He Jingting half-reclined against the sofa, his eyes closed as he took shallow breaths.

The numbers on his phone screen passed midnight. The words Lidong — Beginning of Winter displayed on the system calendar disappeared with the changing date.

Even though he deliberately tried not to think about it, sleep was impossible that night. At times, he drifted into a haze; he sank into unconsciousness, dragged by nightmares as he tumbled down into an ever-deeper abyss.

He had been fifteen that year. It was the first time he learned that his mother was still alive.

After begging everyone he could think of for help, Uncle Chen, the family's driver, finally softened and went through several contacts to find out Shen Yuying’s whereabouts.

After giving birth to him and leaving him at the gate of the He family's ancestral residence, she had dropped out of university and gone north to work.

To his surprise, two years earlier, she had returned to her hometown in a county town near Nanshi.

On a filthy, cramped street lined with night-market stalls stood a tiny noodle shop, barely large enough for a few people.

Under the dim, cheap yellow light, Shen Yuying's once beautiful face had been worn down by years of hardship.

Her waist-length hair was gathered up with a claw clip, and in her hand she held a slotted ladle even thicker than her slender arm, stirring the broth in the steaming pot of noodles.

The two dark-green teardrop earrings swayed gently from side to side with her movements.

Yet there was almost always a smile on her face—for the customers eating their noodles, for the children playing nearby, and even for the tall, dark-skinned man beside her.

The man was constantly busy rolling out dough, chopping vegetables, and greeting customers. In the bleak autumn wind, he repeatedly wiped the sweat from his brow.

The teenage boy hid behind a trash bin in the narrow alley across the street, staying there for an entire night.

He watched them chat and laugh as they closed the shop, watched the way they leaned against each other as they walked home together. He noticed Shen Yuying's slightly protruding belly; the tall stool the man had specially made for her to sit on while he cooked noodles and handled the cash register. Every day, he made sure it was wiped spotless.

Back then, when Shen Yuying had left her hometown as a young woman, he had been burdened by his asthma. Leaving him with the He family had been understandable.

But what about now? Would she want to see him?

For three consecutive days, the boy crouched there until his knees became numb and swollen. Yet he never found the courage to approach her.

What he didn't know was that his supposedly secret whereabouts had already been discovered.

Then came Lidong, the beginning of winter. There were many customers at the noodle shop that day, and Shen Yuying and her husband were so busy that they didn't finish closing up until the early hours of the morning.

After the shop had closed, only a small lamp remained lit in the kitchen. Shen Yuying sat on the tall stool while her husband massaged her aching lower back, the two of them wrapped in a quiet intimacy that was warm and tender.

He Jingting watched silently from a distance. Without realizing it, he began imagining when the younger brother or sister in his mother's womb would be born.

Just then, a blinding pair of headlights came speeding down the main road.

A fully loaded heavy truck swayed dangerously from side to side before hurtling straight toward the noodle shop across the street.

Then—A deafening crash shattered the darkness. Steel and concrete collapsed with a thunderous roar. The tiny noodle shop, barely ten square meters in size, was instantly crushed beyond recognition, reduced to a heap of rubble.

Blood spread across the ruins, trickling drop by drop between the shattered debris. And all that remained before the boy's eyes was a vast, empty whiteness. He stood there in a daze, unable even to breathe.

The frantic screams of the crowd rang in his ears. The wail of the fire engines seemed to circle endlessly through his mind, like a death knell coming from another world.

The ambulance never came.

"How tragic... The crash was so bad there wasn't even a body left intact. And there was a baby in her belly..."

"I heard the truck driver had terminal pancreatic cancer. He was going to die anyway, but he took three lives with him. What a terrible fate..."

......

"Wasn't the truck driver who died in that accident a gambling addict? How could his wife and child possibly have enough money to go abroad?"

"Tsk, tsk. You don't know the whole story. The woman who was killed had once given birth to a son for the He family in Nanshi. There's no way it was that simple..."

A sharp pain suddenly exploded in He Jingting's chest, shooting straight up through his sternum and into his head.

A muffled groan caught in his throat. In the midst of his delirium, he suddenly bit through his lower lip, and the metallic taste of blood spread across his mouth.

The pain left him gasping. His body curled unconsciously into itself, trembling violently. Cold sweat had been pouring from him for who knew how long, soaking through the back of his shirt and dampening his neck.

But his nerves felt as though they were being torn apart. No matter how desperately he struggled, he couldn't wake up.

Filthy gravel scattered everywhere. Roaring flames. Chaotic, overlapping voices… They replayed over and over in his ears, like scenes from a broken reel of film.

In a fit of desperation, He Jingting slammed his fist against his chest. The sudden, excruciating pain finally dragged him completely out of the nightmare.

His vision remained unfocused for a long while. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling windows, the city had blurred into countless flickering points of light. His heart was pumping blood rapidly and erratically.

He grabbed his collar and struggled to breathe, but his lungs felt as though they had been trapped beneath an invisible net, unable to break free.

If it weren't for him… Shen Yuying would have lived a good life, wouldn't she?

It was only many years later that he finally understood.

Back then, that wealthy and carefree young heir had deceived an innocent female university student who knew nothing of the ways of the world. He had not only become the darkest stain on Shen Yuying's life—he had also brought a fatal disaster upon her.

After the heavy truck crashed, it caught fire, burning the bodies and remains to ashes. There wasn't even enough left for a proper burial mound.

And he had never had the right to pay his respects. He hated everyone in this world who bore the surname He. Including himself.

As though punishing himself, He Jingting wrapped his hand around his own throat, cutting off the desperate rush of air. Red marks immediately appeared against his pale, almost frosted skin. His expression was fierce. His fingers tightened harder and harder. His cyanotic lips parted slightly as his body trembled uncontrollably.

Yet this intense, agonizing sensation of suffocation somehow brought him a strange sense of comfort.

The lights before his eyes flickered. In his hazy consciousness, he seemed to become his sixteen-year-old self again, curled helplessly on the floor, fighting for his life as an asthma attack tore through him.

The oxygen around him grew thinner and thinner. The noise surrounding him gradually faded into an icy silence.

"Miss, Master and Madam haven't called back. No one is allowed to take him to the hospital."

"Didn’t you all see? He's dying!" It was the trembling, tearful voice of a little girl. Her hands were pressed against his chest, awkwardly attempting to perform compressions.

It was useless...

At the moment he had resigned himself to death, a white porcelain vase suddenly came crashing to the floor.

Crash!

The sound was sharp and piercing. Through his darkening, blurred vision, his eyes barely opened and closed.

A slender figure stood in front of him, blocking his view. It was the little girl who normally kept her head bowed in front of her father and stepmother, who never even dared to speak back. But in her panic, she had picked up a shard of porcelain and slashed it across her own wrist.

"I'm going to die soon, too! Will you call an ambulance now? Call the ambulance!"

Outside the window, snow fell heavily from the sky. He felt as though he, too, had become a single snowflake—without thought or sensation, drifting helplessly through the wind in a world of absolute silence...

The hand around his throat gradually loosened. He Jingting seemed lost for a moment before collapsing forward onto the sofa, coughing violently.

Then something red caught his eye. He stared at it in a daze for a while before suddenly pulling the red paper bag containing the Decheng pastries into his arms.

The egg rolls and butterfly cookies were packed in metal tins, while the transparent plastic container holding the egg tarts had been carelessly pressed underneath them by the assistant. With trembling hands, He Jingting pulled it out and placed it carefully on top.

He breathed in shallow, uneven gasps. Without meaning to, his mind conjured up the adorable sight of that little girl eating pastries.

She had always been afraid of dropping crumbs in the house and getting scolded, so she would secretly sneak out to the swing in the backyard of the old residence to eat them.

She rarely smiled. Her delicate brows were usually slightly lowered, and her lips were lightly pursed, making her look like a cautious little rabbit.

And yet, surrounded by lush greenery, she was perfectly visible from his third-floor window. Sitting alone on the swing, she would gently swing her feet back and forth. Light seemed to gather in her beautiful eyes.

She would take one tiny bite after another, savoring each mouthful as though she were tasting some priceless treasure. She could smile, too.

He Jingting bent deeply forward and rested his forehead against the cold metal tin. His unfocused gaze gradually softened, like a deep river flowing silently through the darkness of night. His consciousness grew increasingly heavy.

His face became paler and paler. Yet it was as though he had finally touched the air he needed to survive. His expression slowly settled into calm.

"Where does it hurt? He Jingting, wake up!"

"Bring the medicine kit over! Hurry!"

It sounded as though someone was calling his name. But gradually, he could no longer hear anything at all.
