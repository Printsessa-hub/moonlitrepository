A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 03

Moving House

“Sorry, I hope you didn't get burned.” Lu Siyan snapped back to his senses and quickly grabbed a tissue to wipe away the water. “I was just a little surprised—it happened so fast.”

Shu Cheng: “Yeah, we skipped all the red tape.”

His surprise was perfectly understandable.

Arranged marriages between prominent families are incredibly complicated. From the initial mutual interest to the announcement of the engagement, the engagement ceremony, and the wedding itself, the process can take anywhere from a few months to a year or two—and there are plenty of cases where engagements are called off along the way.

He Jingting was the only exception; he suggested getting their marriage certificate right there at the signing ceremony. She still remembers him closing his fountain pen, calmly glancing at his watch: "I have four hours before my flight to Germany, let’s stop by the Civil Affairs Bureau on the way."

And so, Shu Cheng hastily went back to the family home to get her ID card and household registration book, and they got married faster than renewing her passport. The dust has already settled, giving her no chance to back out.

"When is the wedding?"

"Next month." Shu Cheng said with a calm smile. She paused, as if to reassure herself "Actually, this is quite good. The Shu family is... not what it used to be."

On the twentieth floor, the deep sunset painted the crumbling city red, stretching all the way to the other end of the horizon.

Lu Siyan gazed at the girl's profile; her eyelashes drooped slightly, also tinged with a layer of twilight, gradually overlapping with the shy, smiling face he remembered from her first visit to the Lu residence…

For people like them, marriage is always beyond their control, but because it was her, he never resisted. He had initially thought they had plenty of time and could take things slowly.

Dinner was a fairly relaxed affair; the two of them talked about their childhoods, and laughter filled the air.

After dinner, Lu Siyan politely offered to drive Shu Cheng home; not wanting to be too formal, she didn’t refuse.

The car sped along the elevated highway, with brightly lit buildings lining both sides. Shu Cheng rolled down the window, and the slightly cool early-autumn night breeze rushed in, scattering her long, jet-black hair as it fluttered in the wind.

Her gaze, fixed on the distance, seemed to hold a pool of water, shimmering amidst the sweeping lights.

Lu Siyan slowed the car, reluctant to let the moment pass so quickly:

“Are you going to Beichuan next week, too?”

“Beichuan?”

“I'm attending Wan Heng’s charity gala on my father's behalf, and I heard He Jingting will also be there.”

At galas like this, it’s customary to bring a date.

“Oh.” Shu Cheng casually tucked her hair behind her ear. “I probably won’t go—after all, the wedding hasn’t happened yet.”

Her expression was impassive; completely unconcerned which female celebrity showed up arm-in-arm with He Jingting, but then a thought suddenly struck her—

He won’t be in Nanshi next week?

“When is the banquet?,” she pressed.

“The 9th.”

Even the longest road has an end. A dozen minutes later, the car slowly pulled into the residential complex.

Lanwan Peninsula—the apartment complex where Shu Cheng had lived for several years. She preferred to have peace and quiet, so she’d specifically chosen the West District, far from the city center, yet close to her studio.

She got out of the car and said goodbye: “Thanks for the ride. Go home and get some rest.”

Unexpectedly, Lu Siyan opened his door and followed her down.

This quiet, secluded neighborhood really didn’t seem like the kind of place He Jingting would choose for his wedding home. He hesitated for a few seconds, as if he wanted to say something, but knew it wasn’t appropriate to press the issue.

Shu Cheng asked, “What’s wrong?”

Watching her receding figure, he wondered when they would meet again. As if trying to hold on to something, Lu Siyan gathered his thoughts and asked gently, “The company is planning to produce an animated film in the second half of the year. Would you be interested in serving as an art consultant?”

“Me, as a consultant?”

“It’s a supernatural fantasy film, similar in style to treasure hunting in another world,” he explained. “So we’ll need a lot of jewelry designs.”

Shu Cheng, who already felt guilty about the engagement, immediately agreed: “Of course. My entire studio is ready at a moment’s notice.”

The warm yellow glow of the streetlights made her smile even more radiant.

Meanwhile, a black Bentley was hidden in the shadows of the trees around the distant corner, and a piercing gaze shot straight toward her, landing on the girl’s smiling face and the young man standing beside her.

Shu Cheng, completely unaware, said goodbye to Lu Siyan, politely watched his car drive away, and only then turned to go upstairs.

Three minutes later, the light came on in the window on the sixteenth floor.

She took a quick shower, changed into her loungewear, and flopped onto the soft, large bed. A tiny, snow-white kitten leaped up after her, meowing affectionately as it burrowed into her arms.

This was the most soothing moment after a tiring day. Shu Cheng rubbed her cheek against the kitten: “Good girl, Tuantuan.”

Yet, alone in the silence, a faint sense of melancholy once again settled over her heart—

She was far from ready to move into Yujiang Mansion.

Her last experience trying on wedding dresses hadn’t been pleasant, and after hearing what Lu Siyan had said today, she found herself even more reluctant to face He Jingting directly.

Shu Cheng opened her phone, stared at the phone number for a long time, and finally sent a single line: [Is it okay if I move in on the 9th?]

Ten minutes later, a cold, terse reply came back: [Pick another day.]

[I’ve been on a business trip lately, and the 9th is the only day I’m free. Could you please ask the butler or the housekeeper to let me in?]

Soon, a string of numbers came back: [670531]

The door code.

Shu Cheng lifted the kitten above her head, gazing into its beautiful blue eyes, and felt a little lighter inside.

It was just one more day to get through—one day at a time.

The night was deep and pitch-black. One by one, the lights in the apartment building went out, but the Bentley remained parked in the shadows, not having moved an inch.

The lights inside the car were off; the darkness was oppressive, with only the moonlight faintly outlining the sharp silhouette of the man in the back seat. The wind rustled through the leaves, and in the silence, the sound of slightly labored breathing came and went, now fast, now slow.

Finally, the light on the sixteenth floor went out as well.

He Jingting slowly averted his gaze and leaned back against the seat, letting his tension melt away. He tugged at his tight tie and tilted his head back slightly; sharp creases formed where his elbows pressed against the suit jacket.

A small white pill bottle lay on the seat, its cap only half-screwed on, resting loosely.

It felt as if a swarm of tiny, dense termites were gnawing inside his skull slowly and deliberately tormenting every nerve.

The pain made his chest feel increasingly tight, and he pressed his temples with a touch of irritation: “Drive. Take me back to Yujiang Mansion.”

Secretary Zhong spoke cautiously: “President He, Dr. Chen said…”

“Return to Yujiang.”

The order was sharp and unquestionable. “Also, contact Wan Heng and cancel the itinerary for the evening of the 9th.”

The Bentley slowly pulled away, disappearing into the boundless night.

He Jingting closed his eyes tightly and took two deep breaths, trying to ease the stinging pain.

On the warm, navy-blue enamel surface of the watch on his left wrist, the second hand ticked along, one mark at a time. His fingertips traced the cold bezel, repeatedly rubbing a tiny protrusion on the back of the watch against his skin, as if it were his only solace in his pain.

Upon closer inspection, a single sparkling diamond was set in that hidden corner.

He Jingting raised his hand and brought it to his nose. It was as if he could still smell the scent of her hair.

　*

Living under someone else’s roof, Shu Cheng hadn’t packed much—just seasonal clothing and a few daily necessities.

He Jingting was busy with work and was often featured in the news headlines as he traveled all over the world; living in mutual respect was a luxury she dared not even dream of—she only hoped to live as an invisible presence.

On the 9th, fearing he might not have left for Hong Kong yet, Shu Cheng waited until 8:30 p.m. before driving to Yujiang Mansion.

The investment conference there had already begun; even if he’d traveled by rocket, there was no way he’d still be at home.

Just past 9:00 p.m., Shu Cheng’s car was stopped right in front of the security booth.

Yujiang Mansion was the most famous luxury estate in Binjiang, located in the bustling downtown area, and security there was extremely tight.

“I really am He Jingting’s friend,” she said earnestly as she rolled down the window. “I can register my ID, or I can leave it with you as guarantee.”

“I’ve seen over twenty young girls like you this month alone!” the security guard waved her off. “There’s nothing wrong with being a young fan, but don’t disrupt people’s normal lives.”

With that, he slammed the window shut.

Quite a few celebrities lived in this complex, and it wasn’t uncommon for fans to try to sneak in—but…

The security guard glanced at the girl outside, who looked demure and fair-skinned—she was the first person he’d ever seen dare to claim to be a friend of Mr. He from Yunshang.

Accustomed to all kinds of luxury cars, the white BMW before him seemed unremarkable.

They were an elite team, taking great care of every resident. Over the past few years, Mr. He on the 35th floor had never had any visitors other than his secretary and doctor.

The security guard hummed a tune proudly—his sharp wit and dedication had just saved the resident a lot of trouble.

A drizzle began to fall that night, and the autumn breeze carried a chill.

Seeing that her explanations were falling on deaf ears, Shu Cheng had no choice but to pull the car over to the side of the road. Without an umbrella, she ran through the rain to the narrow eaves of the security booth and peered inside: “Sir, I really am—”

He Jingting—that hands-off boss—knew she was moving in but didn’t even bother to give the security guard a heads-up!

Seeing that she wasn’t leaving, the security guard reluctantly opened the window again: “If you really are Mr. He’s friend, have him come pick you up, or have him call us to let us know, and we’ll let you in right away.”

Call him?

Shu Cheng dismissed the idea immediately. He was attending a bidding meeting—there was no guarantee he’d even answer. Wouldn’t that be asking for trouble?

“You’d better go, young lady,” the security guard sighed, speaking earnestly.

“Do you have any idea who Mr. He is? How dare you just pretend to be him? Don’t worry, I’ll pretend this never happened. Just go.”

As the rain grew heavier, Shu Cheng’s left shoulder was already soaked.

She hurriedly blocked the window as it was about to close, taking a desperate gamble: “Actually, I’m not Mr. He’s friend—I’m his wife…”

The security guard gave her a look as if she were an idiot, showing no sign of slowing down as he closed the window.

“It’s true. Why don’t you look up the news photos from a couple of days ago?” she pleaded pitifully. “He’s currently attending an event in Hong Kong and can’t answer his phone. Otherwise, I would’ve had him contact you right away.”

The security guard recalled a headline he’d seen a few days earlier and, half-believing it, half-doubting it, opened his phone. The media photo immediately caught his eye.

Against the dim light of the rain, he looked at the young girl once more. She had a pair of large, crystal-clear eyes and fair skin, her features hidden in the hazy night. Upon closer inspection, she was indeed the same person as in the news photo!

The security guard was so startled he nearly fainted.

“I’m so sorry, Mrs. He, my apologies—” He hurriedly ushered her inside, fetching a towel and pouring her some hot tea. “Please forgive me, there are quite a few celebrities living in this complex, and we often have young girls coming by to chase celebrities. You look so young; you really look just like one of those college students.”

This form of address felt very unfamiliar, and Shu Cheng smiled awkwardly: “No need to go to all that trouble. Could you tell me how to get to the underground parking garage?”

The security guard quickly made a call, asking a colleague to come help her park, carry her luggage, and escort her all the way to the lobby.

Every building at Yujiang Mansion features a 24-hour hotel-style service lobby. With a ceiling height exceeding six meters, it was spacious and bright, adorned with magnificent crystal chandeliers hanging high—a sight that was every bit as impressive as any five-star hotel in the city center, leaving one speechless.

The lobby concierge swiped her card, and Shu Cheng smoothly took the elevator up to the thirty-fifth floor.

From the elevator lobby to the front door, the light was a stark white, illuminating the dark gray, finely veined natural marble floor. Nothing was placed there; the spotless cleanliness carried a hint of coldness.

Usually, someone as busy as He Jingting would have a butler or housekeeper at home, wouldn’t they?

Shu Cheng pressed the doorbell twice first, but there was still no answer from inside, so she entered the six-digit code she had received earlier.

“Beep—”

The sound of the lock clicking open echoed through the empty hallway.

Shu Cheng carefully pushed the door open and peered inside.

The room was pitch black; the hallway light only faintly illuminated the sideboard in the entryway. She couldn’t make out anything else—it was as if a black hole had swallowed all the light.

“It’s fine that no one’s home,” she thought. She carried the two suitcases into the entryway and, using the faint light from outside, felt along the wall for the light switch.

Just as she took two steps inside, the hallway light suddenly went out.

Everything plunged into darkness. Shu Cheng called out twice, but the hallway didn’t light up again.

Wasn’t it a motion-sensor light? It had been on when the elevator doors opened just a moment ago.

Shu Cheng wasn’t usually afraid of the dark, but in this unfamiliar house, her eyes hadn’t fully adjusted to the surroundings yet, and her vision was like a blank void.

It was pouring rain outside, and the darkness felt like a surging deep sea, sending a chill down her spine as the “thump, thump, thump” of her heartbeat grew louder and louder.

“The switch… where on earth did he put the switch…”

Shu Cheng muttered under her breath to steel her nerves as she continued feeling her way along the cold wall. In her extreme anxiety, she momentarily forgot to use her phone as a flashlight.

Suddenly, a deep, cold male voice rang out behind her: “It’s here.” The voice was so close it felt like a ghostly whisper creeping into her ear.

Shu Cheng shuddered in fright and let out a shriek: “Ah—”

At that very moment, there was a “click.” Dim light flared up from the ceiling and at her feet.

Shu Cheng turned around, her heart pounding with fear. The ambient lighting didn’t fully illuminate the entryway; it merely cast a faint outline of a tall man’s figure, who stood a step away, gazing down at her coldly.

The white light swept across his deep-set brow and the bridge of his nose, casting mottled shadows on his face and carving it into blocks of cold, resolute expression.

He Jingting.

Shu Cheng’s heart skipped a beat, and in her shock, she blurted out, “What are you doing here!”

“Why would I be in my own home?” He gave a soft chuckle, tinged with a hint of sarcasm. “Or… did you think I wasn’t here?”

This was the first time she’d ever seen He Jingting smile, and it sent a shiver down her spine even more than his anger ever had. The glint that flashed in his eyes was like the cold light of a poisoned blade just before it leaves its sheath.

Shu Cheng unconsciously clenched the hem of her shirt, her eyelashes fluttering rapidly as she dared not answer his question.

He Jingting didn’t wait for her reply. He leaned forward slightly and reached out to touch her cheek, gently brushing it with the slightly rough pad of his finger.

His fingertips were ice-cold and slightly damp, like the temperature of a dead person.

Feeling the slight tremor of the profile in his hand, He Jingting murmured four words with satisfaction: “Are you avoiding me?” It was like a cold breath from hell.
