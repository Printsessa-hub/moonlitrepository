A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 06

Concealment

After a good night’s sleep, Shu Cheng woke up to find the bed next to her empty.

The curtains were drawn tight, and the room was still dark. She turned on her phone and realized she’d slept until 11:00 a.m. Without bothering to open the window, she hopped out of bed barefoot, pushed the bedroom door open a crack, and poked her head out.

Bright light flooded in. Shu Cheng blinked, and the man standing in the living room was already looking her way.

Their eyes met.

“Come here.” He Jingting leisurely fastened his wristwatch and adjusted the collar of his suit.

Despite working nonstop for over ten hours yesterday, he still looked very energetic today. He was wearing a double-breasted suit with a notched lapel; the navy blue looked impeccably sharp on him, as if he were heading to a business event.

Shu Cheng tugged at her hair and obediently walked over.

He glanced at her feet: “Put on your shoes.”

“Oh.”

She was still a little groggy, so she hurried back to her bedroom to put on her slippers.

In just over ten seconds, He Jingting was already standing at the front door—it seemed she’d woken up at a truly inopportune moment.

“If you need to make an internal call, meal delivery and housekeeping services are available here 24 hours a day.” He glanced at his watch and asked casually, “Where will you be tomorrow afternoon?”

“I’ll probably be at the nursing home.”

Her maternal grandmother was the only true relative she had left in this world. She’d been bedridden and recuperating ever since her heart failure surgery five years ago, and Shu Cheng visited her at the nursing home almost every Thursday.

“Why didn’t you tell me sooner?” he asked. “I’m going on a business trip.”

Shu Cheng was taken aback and blurted out, “It’s okay, I’ll just go by myself.” Before she could finish speaking, He Jingting furrowed his brow.

He said nothing, pausing briefly as he held his briefcase in his left hand, revealing the back of his hand—strong, bulging veins. A platinum wedding ring glistened on his ring finger, casting a warm gleam in the sunlight.

Feeling a twinge of guilt, she looked away. As newlyweds, they were supposed to visit their elders together.

But her grandmother was frail and couldn’t handle any emotional stimulation; her state of mind had to remain stable. Moreover, since she’d watched Lu Siyan grow up and doted on him, Shu Cheng had never revealed that she’d broken off her engagement with the Lu family and had instead married someone else in a whirlwind wedding.

“Actually, I haven’t told Grandma that we’re married yet,” Shu Cheng said timidly. “It all happened a bit too suddenly…”

From their first meeting to getting their marriage license to the wedding—it had all happened in less than two months.

He Jingting raised his hand to loosen his tie, his expression hidden in the shadows, making it hard to read: “When do you plan to tell her?”

She stared at her toes and asked hesitantly, “Can we hold off for now? Grandma’s in a hospital room with no access to the internet or the news, and the doctors and nurses will keep it confidential. She has a weak heart and can’t handle any stress…”

If she could, she’d even want to keep it from her forever.

The man in front of her turned toward her, his tone suddenly turning cold: “Stress.”

As he uttered those two words, it was as if a fire were burning deep within his dark eyes, searing through all her hidden, secret thoughts.

“Is it because of the sudden marriage—or because it’s to me?”

If she were marrying Lu Siyan, would the news of their marriage also be something to hide like this?

Shu Cheng immediately realized she’d said too much: “No, that’s not it…”

But as she opened her mouth, she couldn’t even come up with an excuse on the spot and gently bit her lower lip.

He Jingting continued to look down at her expressionlessly until the lingering silence became another kind of answer.

He didn’t say another word and simply turned away.

The front door closed sharply in front of her, leaving behind a deathly silence.

Only then did Shu Cheng realize she’d forgotten to ask what he’d originally wanted to discuss with her tomorrow afternoon.

Bright morning light flooded the living room, and everything returned to calm. Feeling inexplicably down, she let out a soft sigh, casually tied her hair back, and went to the bathroom to wash up.

The running water formed a small whirlpool in the sink, and her gaze inadvertently fell on the mirror. The towel rack behind her was completely empty. Where was her hair cap?

　*

Over the next few days, He Jingting left early and returned late; sometimes he hadn’t come home by the time Shu Cheng had fallen asleep.

That unspoken matter had faded into thin air. Though they lived under the same roof, they hardly ever crossed paths—like two distant parallel lines.

The suit jacket draped in the living room in the mornings would occasionally carry a faint scent of alcohol; after the butler took it to be pressed, it looked as good as new, as if no trace of him had ever been there.

Not having to exchange awkward glances made things a little easier for Shu Cheng. Aside from visiting her grandmother at the nursing home, she returned to the apartment every day to play with Tuantuan for a while.

Unable to fathom his thoughts, she didn’t dare bring the kitten to Yujiang Mansion on a whim. Several times she’d wanted to ask, but the words wouldn’t come out. She’d drafted the text message countless times, only for her finger to freeze over the “Send” button each time.

Ever since she was little, she’d been invisible at home. Even when she wanted a new backpack, she could only bring it up with trepidation. When her father was in a good mood, everything was fine, but when his business wasn’t going well, she’d face scolding at best—and beatings at worst…

Over time, she became terrified of “asking” for anything.

“Tuantuan, I’m sorry for making you a ‘left-behind’ kitten…” Shu Cheng stroked the fluffy white ball in her arms. Seeing that it was almost 9:00 p.m., she reluctantly got to her feet.

He Jingting had never come home before 11:00 p.m., so she wasn’t in a hurry. She slowly parked her car in the garage and went upstairs.

She entered the code and casually opened the door—

To her surprise, the living room was brightly lit. He Jingting was leaning against the sofa, and standing across from him at the coffee table was a young man she didn’t recognize.

As soon as the door opened, the man’s gaze swept over to her—it was a very familiar face. He had refined, distant features, wore a pair of elegant gold-rimmed glasses, and exuded a strong scholarly air.

Shu Cheng remembered that it was Dr. Chen, whom she’d met in the lounge on her wedding day. Since he was serving as the best man, he was likely a personal friend of He Jingting’s.

She nodded politely, changed her shoes, and went to the bathroom to wash her hands. As she came out, she was suddenly called back.

“Miss Shu, I’m sorry, but I have to leave now,” Chen Yanqing said politely. “Please make sure he takes these two medications again in half an hour.” He picked up two aluminum foil blister packs: “ Half a white one, and three round ones.”

Only then did Shu Cheng notice that He Jingting, sitting on the sofa, looked unwell—his eyes were half-closed, and his lips were pale. He hadn’t even taken off his suit yet; he was leaning back slightly, his arms tense and pressed against his chest, as if trying to suppress his discomfort.

Before she could ask, he said impatiently, “I’ll take them myself.”

Chen Yanqing ignored him and continued to instruct her calmly, “It’s best not to let him take a shower within the next two hours; as the dilation of blood vessels will make the dizziness worse.”

Shu Cheng nodded in agreement, but she was still completely confused: “Um… what’s wrong with him?” She couldn’t tell what was wrong with him either.

A hint of surprise flashed in Chen Yanqing’s eyes. Just as he was about to speak, he was interrupted with a hint of annoyance.

He Jingting was unsparing: “Aren’t you supposed to catch a flight?” Chen Yanqing chuckled wryly—just moments ago, he’d been in too much pain to speak, but now that this young lady was back, he’d suddenly found his energy.

Being too familiar with his friend’s temperament, he glanced at his watch, quickly gathered his things, and left.

As he passed through the doorway, he smiled at Shu Cheng, who was standing there dumbfounded, and said thoughtfully, “He has a bit of a headache. A cold compress before bed can help relieve the pain. I’d appreciate your help with that.”

With repeated ‘apologies for any trouble” there, Shu Cheng began to feel awkward and quickly agreed, “It’s no trouble at all, Dr. Chen. Have a safe trip.”

As soon as the front door closed, silence fell once more over the living room.

Shu Cheng paced back to the sofa and saw He Jingting still leaning back in the same position as before, resting with his eyes closed, showing no intention of speaking.

Her gaze fell on the two boxes of medicine, and she suddenly remembered something; she hurried out after him.

Fortunately, Chen Yanqing had just reached the lobby, and Shu Cheng took another elevator to catch up with him: “Please wait a moment.”

“Miss Shu, is there something I can help you with?”

He was charming, gentle, and polite—and between the titles “Miss Shu” and “Mrs. He,” Shu Cheng particularly preferred the former.

Not wanting to be too direct, she came up with a pretext: “Those white painkillers from earlier—aren’t they an original-brand medication that just went on the market in the U.S. last year? I’ve heard they’re hard to come by. My grandmother suffers from joint pain—would this medication be suitable for the elderly?”

“They’re quite effective,” Chen Yanqing replied briefly. “I have two more boxes at home; I’ll bring them to you next time so you can try them.”

After thanking him, Shu Cheng showed no sign of leaving. “Dr. Chen, I have another question I’d like to ask you…”

Chen Yanqing raised an eyebrow; he knew the real reason for her visit was yet to come.

She hesitated over her words, but her mouth spoke before her thoughts could catch up: “Do you know how anything about his health… in other areas?”

He asked, “ In which aspects?”

“No, no,” Shu Cheng stammered, quickly correcting herself. “I meant to ask if his asthma has improved? From a medical professional’s perspective, do you think it’s okay for us to have a pet at home?”

A turtle? A rabbit? That would depend on the specifics. Chen Yanqing remained silent, quietly waiting for her to elaborate.

“For example…” Shu Cheng’s voice trailed off, lacking confidence. “A kitten or something like that.”

“Although Jingting and I are friends,” Chen Yanqing said with an apologetic smile, “I cannot disclose any information about his health to anyone. You’ll have to ask him directly.”

…

After beating around the bush like that, why didn’t he just say right away that he couldn’t disclose it?

She was at a loss for words, sensing that this Dr. Chen wasn’t quite as easy to get along with as he appeared to be.

“All right, thanks.” Having been turned away, Shu Cheng had no choice but to slink back upstairs.

When she returned upstairs, the living room was empty, and the sound of running water came from the bathroom in the master bedroom. Shu Cheng paused in surprise. Hadn’t he just said he couldn’t shower for two hours because he might faint?

Chen Yanqing had left less than ten minutes ago and was already defying his doctor’s orders—no wonder he needed someone to make sure he took his medicine.

The bathroom door was tightly shut; He Jingting was definitely inside. Hesitating, she knocked lightly on the door twice: “Are you alright?”

There was no reply. The sound of the shower fluctuated between soft and loud, then stopped after a while, but he didn’t come out for a long time.

He couldn’t have really fainted in there, could he?

Shu Cheng moved closer to the bathroom door, but the frosted glass blocked her view—she couldn’t see a thing. She didn’t have the guts to open the door directly, so she could only press her ear against it…

Suddenly, the door swung open from the inside. Shu Cheng jumped in surprise and nearly bumped into He Jingting’s chest.

“What are you doing?” A cold, detached voice came from above her.

The tips of her hair accidentally brushed against the collar of his pajamas; they were mere inches apart. The crisp scent of his shower gel washed over her, carrying a faint hint of moisture.

Shu Cheng took half a step back and awkwardly looked away: “…The doctor said you shouldn’t take a shower.”

He Jingting’s brows furrowed slightly; his face was completely bloodless, appearing even paler beneath his dark, wet hair.

Seeing her quickly put distance between them, he snorted impatiently: “It’s not like I’m going to die.”

As soon as he finished speaking, he walked straight past her toward the living room. In that split second as they brushed past each other, a sharp pain exploded across his forehead.

He Jingting staggered, a heavy gasp escaping his lips as he began to fall forward.

“Whoa—” Shu Cheng instinctively grabbed his arm. Her hand met an icy chill that seeped through the thin fabric of his nightshirt into her palm; his entire body radiated coldness.

She was stunned. To avoid aggravating his headache, he’d actually taken a cold shower. But it was already late autumn, and the nighttime temperature outside was in the single digits; how could his body possibly withstand such an ordeal?

For a split moment, He Jingting’s vision was reduced to flickering spots of light, and the world spun around him. The excruciating pain felt like a sharp knife piercing right through the top of his head.

“Ugh…” He forced back the nausea rising in his throat, and cold sweat instantly soaked his temples. If she hadn’t been holding him up, he might have already collapsed to the floor.

“Can you still walk?”

Unable to support He Jingting’s height—he was over 1.8 meters tall—Shu Cheng was already teetering on the verge of collapse; she tried to take half a step toward the bedroom.

She had never imagined anyone could suffer from a headache this severe, yet she was still startled by his deathly pale complexion.

“Wait—” Don’t move.

He Jingting’s Adam’s apple bobbed with difficulty as he struggled to raise his hand to brace himself against the wall, taking some of his weight off her.

It felt as if molten lead were pouring into his temples; the searing pain shot down his nerves. He’d almost lost all sensation except for the pain—he couldn’t even draw a breath.

“Okay, okay.” Feeling his whole body tremble, Shu Cheng didn’t dare take another step forward. This was probably the closest she’d ever been to He Jingting, but her momentary panic had made her forget just how intimate this position was.

After what felt like an eternity, the white light before his eyes slowly faded, and he finally exhaled: “All right…”

Shu Cheng helped him onto the bed to rest, then went to the living room to fetch some warm water and medicine.

From a distance, in the soft light, He Jingting was half-reclining against the headboard. His usually sharp features were now downcast, his jaw slightly clenched with restraint; he still looked very uncomfortable.

Shu Cheng had intended to set the medicine box on the nightstand, but after a moment’s thought, she pulled out a strip instead. Following Chen Yanqing’s instructions, she broke off three tablets and handed them to him along with the glass of water.

“Here, take your medicine first.” He Jingting took them silently and swallowed them with water. The other dose was half a tablet, but the oval pill was no bigger than a grain of rice.

Shu Cheng held it between her fingertips, trying to figure out how to break it into two even halves, and remarked casually: “You definitely shouldn’t take a cold shower when you have a headache—you’re not made of iron, after all.”

A strand of hair slipped from her shoulder and fell beside her ear. The girl leaned forward slightly, her expression focused as she worked on the pill in her hand. Her long eyelashes drooped, and her lips—still coated with a light layer of lip gloss, looking moist and rosy as they parted and closed with each word she spoke.

He Jingting stared at her lips; the pain sparked another, even more violent impulse—to lift her up and pull her into his arms, holding her tightly and claiming her.

His fingertips, buried deep in the comforter, clenched tightly, leaving a crease in the fabric.

But Shu Cheng’s full attention was on her hands: “Besides, the weather has gotten quite cold now, you could catch a cold easily if you do that…”

Thud.

The small pill split successfully in two. When she looked up, she saw He Jingting staring at her.

His cold eyes were unfathomably dark, seemingly stirring with some dormant emotion.

I’m done for. Shu Cheng shuddered and snapped back to her senses.

She’d gotten carried away. Just because she’d been a medical assistant for a short while, did that really give her the nerve to criticize He Jingting?

“Um, I…” She placed the pill in his hand and said innocently, “You should get some rest.” Shu Cheng turned off the lights and fled in a panic. It wasn’t until much later that He Jingting took his gaze off the doorway.

The room felt empty, and it was as if a piece of his chest were missing—the pain was even worse than before. He closed his eyes weakly,, fumbled for his phone, and opened the chat with Chen Yanqing: [What did she say to you just now?]

On the other end, Chen Yanqing was sitting in a luxury car on the way to the airport. When he saw the message pop up, the corners of his mouth curled up in amusement.

He’s already chasing after me to ask—so this day has finally come for him, too.

【She asked if she could keep a cat at your place.】

A cat? Instead of asking me directly, she went around in circles to ask an outsider? In the darkness, He Jingting narrowed his eyes slightly.
