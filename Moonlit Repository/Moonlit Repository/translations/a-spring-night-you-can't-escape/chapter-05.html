A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 05

Moment of Distraction

The jewelry box she had prepared in advance sat quietly in front of the mirror.

Shu Cheng dared not make any more excuses to avoid him. She declined the makeup artist’s help, lifted the voluminous hem of her dress, and walked alone toward the dressing room at the end of the hallway.

The crystal chandelier above flickered, casting her restless shadow onto the magnificent European-style carpet.

This was her first time seeing He Jingting again since that stormy night.

Secretary Zhong was waiting outside. Upon seeing her, she didn’t open the door immediately, her expression betraying a hint of hesitation.

“President He is…” Secretary Zhong paused, then turned to close the door behind her. “Please wait a moment.”

What a grand display.

Shu Cheng thought to herself as she waited at the door for nearly ten minutes, holding the rather heavy jewelry box, before there was any movement from inside again.

“Madam, please come in.”

As Secretary Zhong opened the door, a man carrying a brown leather briefcase followed her out.

He wore a best man’s boutonniere pinned to the lapel of his suit; with a gentle and refined demeanor, he nodded and smiled at her.

As they passed each other, Shu Cheng caught a faint whiff of disinfectant.

Secretary Zhong showed no intention of going inside and closed the door firmly behind her.

Several wall lamps illuminated the lounge, and the ornate European-style ceiling was covered with mythological murals, making it feel as though she had stumbled into an ancient Greek oil painting.

By the floor-to-ceiling windows, He Jingting leaned casually against a red velvet sofa, his eyes half-closed, a rare look of fatigue visible between his brows. He didn’t seem to notice anyone entering; his left elbow rested on the armrest, and his thumb kept massaging his temple, stroke by stroke.

Shu Cheng’s high heels sank into the carpet as she approached silently.

It wasn’t until she was almost directly in front of He Jingting that he sensed her presence; his hand paused, and he opened his eyes.

His pupils were as black as thick ink, and his sharp gaze swept straight toward her. Without uttering a single word, he exuded an innate sense of intimidation.

Shu Cheng immediately recalled the way he had stared at her intently that night as he closed in on her, and she nervously tugged at the hem of her dress.

The change in her expression didn’t escape He Jingting’s notice; it felt like a sharp thorn piercing his heart.

He covered his mouth and coughed lightly twice, then softened his tone. “Come here and take a look at the wedding jewelry.”

Fortunately, he was sitting on a single armchair, and since Shu Cheng’s train was quite voluminous, she found a spacious sofa nearby to sit on.

This distance put her mind a little more at ease.

“I’ve put together four sets to match the style of wedding dress, plus a spare set—all made with the gemstones Secretary Zhong sent over.”

She opened the first layer of the jewelry box, and from the black velvet fabric emerged a mesmerizing emerald.

Two teardrop-shaped earrings, their golden edges encasing swirling shades of green, resembled elves imprisoned in a forest.

Set after set of jewelry was unveiled: pink diamonds, pigeon’s blood rubies, jade… As these rare treasures passed through her hands, they transformed into a dazzling display of colors.

He Jingting’s gaze swept slowly over them, but he reached out only to pick up that pair of earrings and examine them closely. He gently caressed the warm, pure dark green between his slender fingers; his gaze grew slightly somber, and he furrowed his brow with an inscrutable expression.

Shu Cheng was so anxious she even held her breath, as if she were having her exam paper graded.

The silence was unbearable, so she couldn’t help but speak up to explain: “There were some impurities along the edges of the rough stone, so I cut it into a teardrop shape.”

He Jingting didn’t reply. He suddenly turned his head and coughed a few times, closing his eyes as if feeling slightly unwell. He raised his left hand slightly, then rested it lightly on the armrest.

When he spoke again, his voice was already somewhat hoarse: “Replace this pair.”

Shu Cheng waited a few seconds, as if accustomed to the fact that his decisions required no explanation—then lowered her gaze and put the emeralds away.

They were truly beautiful. In all her years studying jewelry, she had never seen such pure, translucent beryl outside of textbooks.

His face looked a little pale as he added: “Dispose of them. Don’t wear it again.”

Did he dislike them that much? But these raw stones had all been sent by Secretary Zhong; ultimately, they belonged to him.

Shu Cheng looked bewildered. “How should I dispose of them?”

He Jingting waved his hand, signaling the butler to take all the jewelry away for the time being.

“Sell them, give them away, or throw them away,” he said. “It’s up to you.”

As the man remained silent in the lounge, the atmosphere grew tense.

He closed his eyes to rest, showing no intention of speaking again. His breathing was somewhat heavy, and his index finger tapped slowly and gently, once, then twice, on the mahogany armrest, as if he were tired.

Shu Cheng tactfully kept a low profile.

It wasn’t until quite a while later that He Jingting’s expression softened, and he had dinner brought in: “We won’t have time to eat later, so have a little something now.”

The butler quickly brought in an exquisite meal: a plate of translucent matsutake dumplings, a bowl of bird’s nest soup, two cherry-sized pieces of foie gras, and a cup of hot black tea.

Shu Cheng took a sip of the bird’s nest soup; it was sweet and smooth, offering a warm comfort on this late autumn night.

He Jingting, sitting across from her, did not touch his food. He merely lifted his teacup and gazed quietly at her.

She rarely tied her hair up, but now it was styled in a bun, revealing a swan-like neck whenever she lowered her head. Her beautiful eyelashes drooped slightly as she held the small bowl, sipping the bird’s nest soup into her mouth one spoonful at a time; her lips brushed against the porcelain-white spoon, leaving a faint trace of lipstick.

She looked like an adorable little rabbit.

He Jingting unconsciously crossed his left leg over his right knee, suppressing the restlessness within him.

But Shu Cheng was too absorbed in eating to notice the change in the man’s gaze across from her.

It wasn’t until her bowl was empty that He Jingting gently pushed his plate forward a few inches, and it clattered against hers with a “clack.”

Shu Cheng looked up to see that his plate remained untouched, save for a few sips of hot tea.

Was he offering it to her? She was both flattered and taken aback: “Aren’t you going to eat?”

“I’m not hungry.” He gestured for her to take it and eat.

Shu Cheng had already finished her own plate and was actually full, but under his gaze, she obediently ate more than half a bowl.

When she put down her spoon, she noticed He Jingting was still watching her.

She looked at him innocently: “I really am full…”

He finally looked away and said to the butler, “Put it away.”

By then, the estate’s lights had come on, their points of light forming a constellation that cast a warm glow on He Jingting’s silhouette.

Shu Cheng recalled the earlier news about the wedding and seized the moment: “Manager Li from the bridal shop—why don’t we just… send them back to their hometown?”

Yunshang’s stock had soared, and the controversy surrounding the Shu and Lu families had been merely a passing media storm, causing no real losses.

He Jingting didn’t seem surprised: “Do you know how much they sold it for?”

“How much?”

“Five million.”

Hearing that figure, Shu Cheng couldn’t help but be startled—could a single marriage announcement really be worth that much?

He continued, “They originally planned to sell it to Wan Heng.”

Wan Heng Group was Yunshang’s strongest competitor in today’s emerging business landscape. If the initiative truly fell into their hands, the consequences would be impossible to predict.

Shu Cheng was speechless. No wonder the media and public sentiment had been overwhelmingly in Yunshang’s favor—it turned out he had been pulling the strings behind the scenes when the engagement was announced.

As he spoke of this, He Jingting elegantly sipped his black tea, but his gaze suddenly turned cold: “Actually, the fact that they dared to approach you means they haven’t learned their lesson yet.”

His tone was indifferent, yet that final word was lightly bitten between his teeth, as if pronouncing a death sentence on a condemned prisoner.

Shu Cheng couldn’t help but feel a chill creep up from her ankles, slowly, bit by bit, permeating her entire body.

The atmosphere, which had just eased for a moment, felt like an illusion. The cooled snow pear soup remained in the bowl, having congealed into a murky, gelatinous layer.

The business world is full of unpredictable, ever-changing human nature, but for He Jingting, manipulating it all was as easy as crushing an ant. And timid as she was, she didn’t even dare to ask what he intended to do with Manager Li and the other person.

Until the wedding officially began, silence reigned in the lounge.

*

That night, the wedding of the He and Shu families was a grand spectacle, drawing countless admiring glances and envious stares.

Amid the dazzling lights, Shu Cheng smiled in her pure white wedding gown, though her heart was quietly filled with tears.

If… if this were to be her whole life—just like this marriage, a hollow shell beneath a glamorous facade, wouldn’t that be a magnificent tragedy?

As the cool pink diamond ring slid onto her ring finger, her fingertips trembled slightly, only to be firmly grasped by He Jingting’s slightly cool hand.

Suddenly, he leaned down toward her. With a “pop,” the confetti balloons above their heads burst open.

Amid the shower of petals, Shu Cheng’s heart skipped a few beats as she waited for the kiss she had mentally prepared herself for.

However, He Jingting gallantly lifted her hand—the kiss landed gently on the back of Shu Cheng’s hand. A fleeting sensation, moist and cool, as light as a feather brushing past. This wasn’t part of the kissing routine they’d agreed on beforehand…

Loud cheers and applause instantly erupted from the audience below. Shu Cheng looked up in bewilderment, her gaze meeting He Jingting’s dark eyes head-on. His gaze was intense, with a deep current she couldn’t decipher surging beneath the surface, like a silent whirlpool in the night, both fiery and obscure.

She froze for a moment, then quickly looked away.

Petals scattered across the hem of her dress and settled on his shoulders as well.

For this wedding banquet, the ceremony was merely the prelude; the social gathering that followed was the main event.

But this was no longer Shu Cheng’s stage. She had changed into a simple, straight-cut, off-the-shoulder long dress and now followed behind He Jingting, holding a glass of orange juice, serving as a beautiful and graceful accessory.

In the business world, He Jingting’s commanding presence remained as sharp as ever; full of vigor, he engaged in conversation with prominent figures from all walks of life.

Amid the clinking of glasses, he raised his glass time and again, draining it only to have it refilled just as quickly. With each guest’s preferences differing, red wine, white wine, champagne, and cocktails—liquids of various hues swirled in the stemmed glasses.

Shu Cheng disliked such insincere gatherings and had not the slightest regard for businesspeople who played both sides; naturally, she didn’t notice the man beside her growing increasingly pale.

He Jingting’s hand holding the glass wavered slightly; as he tilted his head back, the wine choked him twice before he managed to swallow it.

The real estate developer was still enthusiastically outlining plans for the upcoming amusement park in the western suburbs, and Shu Cheng smiled, her mind drifting slightly.

Her father, Shu Lin’s, ingratiating smile from just moments ago was still fresh in her mind: “Little He, from now on, I’m entrusting our Chengcheng to you.”

“So many years have passed in the blink of an eye. You two have been a perfect match since childhood. It's truly wonderful that you’ve finally tied the knot…”

Her stepmother, Li Lan, had also come with her son; even her heavy makeup couldn’t hide the faint dissatisfaction in her eyes. The three of them stood there, looking as incongruous as a joke.

And that included her, of course.

That was still the better scenario. He’s father had suffered a stroke years ago out of anger when his second son was imprisoned; now, claiming illness, the entire family hadn’t even shown their faces at the wedding banquet—a clear sign that they’d already severed ties.

But He Jingting seemed completely unfazed, as composed as ever. The light cast a faint shadow across his straight shoulders; as he raised his glass, his jawline was sharply defined, and his Adam’s apple moved slightly…

Shu Cheng lowered her gaze. At that moment, “united in appearance but divided in heart” was the most fitting phrase to describe their relationship.

By the time all the guests had left, it was nearly midnight.

After the festivities, only emptiness remained—fresh flower petals had been trampled by countless people, leaving behind a mess of scattered petals and withered leaves.

“Go wait for me in the car.”

He Jingting left only one sentence behind before vanishing without a trace.

Shu Cheng was utterly exhausted and had no energy to ask why; she sat down in the Maybach waiting at the manor to rest.

After standing in high heels for nearly five hours, her ankles were so sore she could no longer feel them. Secretary Zhong thoughtfully asked if she wanted to go back to the dressing room first to change into casual clothes.

“Thank you, but no need. ”

If she were returning to the Yujiang Mansion, it would only be a ten-kilometer drive. But she waited in the car for twenty minutes, and He Jingting still hadn’t returned.

At midnight, the manor was deserted; the lights went out, and the surroundings were suddenly plunged into deep darkness. Shu Cheng rested her forehead against the cold windowpane, a small habit she had when she was feeling down.

Another ten minutes passed before He Jingting finally showed up. The moment he opened the car door, the night breeze swept in, carrying a heavy scent of alcohol.

Shu Cheng hated the smell of alcohol; she instinctively frowned and rolled down the window a few inches. The car immediately pulled away, turned around, and headed toward the main road.

Compared to the lounge or banquet hall, the backseat of the car felt even more cramped and intimate. He Jingting was tall with long legs, and his mere presence there made him impossible to ignore.

Shu Cheng was still shaken by the events of that night. She discreetly shifted closer to the window and pretended to be asleep with her eyes closed to escape the situation.

He Jingting was equally silent, leaning back against the darkness on the other side of the car, struggling to suppress his increasingly heavy breathing.

As was his custom, he had taken his asthma medication both before and after drinking; a large dose of bronchodilators had seeped into his bloodstream, bringing waves of weakness that seemed to seep into his very bones. The persistent headache wasn’t letting him off the hook; on the contrary, the dizziness grew even more unbearable.

It felt as if a heavy hammer were shattering his temples. The veins on his hand, clenched tightly around the doorknob, bulged as he struggled to keep his body from slumping downward.

The pain caused him to lose consciousness several times; out of the corner of his eye, he saw the girl’s silhouette, curled up in a ball and cowering in fear. At that moment, He Jingting couldn’t tell which part of him hurt more; he only knew that the agony was on the verge of tearing his very soul apart.

In the silence, his ragged breathing had become impossible to ignore.

Shu Cheng recalled the rare look of exhaustion on his face before the wedding banquet, as well as the man who had emerged from the lounge—she’d heard others address him as “Dr. Chen”...

Was he sick? Or was he drunk? Should she ask?

She quietly peeked through her eyelids and saw that the silhouette, half-hidden in the darkness, remained motionless. Aside from his chest rising and falling a bit too quickly, there was nothing else out of the ordinary.

Secretary Zhong in the driver’s seat showed no reaction either.

If it had been a stranger, she would certainly have offered her concern, but it was none other than the unpredictable He Jingting—which made her afraid to approach him easily.

Just as Shu Cheng was wrestling with her decision, that sculpted figure stirred first. He Jingting leaned forward slightly and fumbled for a white medicine bottle in the seat’s side pocket. But his hand was shaking so badly that he twisted the cap roughly twice, barely managing to hold onto the tiny bottle.

Suddenly, a sharp pain shot through him, and his fingertips slipped. The bottle fell from his hand and rolled to the edge of Shu Cheng’s beige high heels.

Now she could no longer pretend to be asleep; she bent down to pick up the medicine bottle. It looked like a repackaged medication—there were no labels on the bottle.

Shu Cheng picked it up carefully, but just as she raised her hand, He Jingting snatched it away. He used a bit too much force, and his fingernails dug deeply into the palm of her hand, which felt damp and cold.

She watched as he held his breath to unscrew the cap, poured out a few pills, and swallowed them without even taking a sip of water.

She tentatively asked: “You…”

“It’s fine.” He Jingting cut her off sharply, his tone stiff. He then folded his arms across his chest and closed his eyes.

Outside the window, streetlights swept by, casting flickering light and shadow. Shu Cheng paused for a moment. Seeing his aloof demeanor, she decided not to press the matter further.

Upon arriving at the Yujiang Mansion, He Jingting got out of the car and went upstairs without saying a word. She quietly thanked Secretary Zhong and followed him into the elevator.

This time, they were truly alone together.

Gazing at the man’s back just inches away, Shu Cheng felt a bit nervous and gently clenched her fingers.

She had never been in a relationship, but she knew all too well what their wedding night meant. With a marriage contract in hand, she wasn’t naive enough to think she could refuse his demands.

Coming back to the 35th floor brings a completely different feeling. The front door opened, and the living room lights came on automatically, their warm, white, clean glow illuminating the entire room in layers.

He Jingting changed his shoes, turned, and walked into the master bedroom. Before Shu Cheng could even react, the sound of running water came from the bathroom. From the moment he walked in, he hadn’t even spared her a single glance.

Shu Cheng let out a sigh of relief and slowly slipped off the “torture devices” on her feet, replacing them with the women’s slippers from the shoe cabinet.

The living room was bright and airy; with floor-to-ceiling windows, she could admire the most bustling river view in Nanshi. But she had no heart to look at it right now; the distant sound of running water felt like a merciless countdown, hanging high above her head.

He Jingting was taking a shower in the master bedroom’s bathroom. Shu Cheng tiptoed around the house for quite a while before finally finding the suitcase she’d left behind in the walk-in closet during her last visit.

All her clothes had already been neatly folded and stored in one side of the wardrobe by the housekeeper; every piece had been re-ironed, without a single wrinkle.

Before she realized it, the sounds coming from the master bedroom had fallen silent.

Shu Cheng walked over, clutching her pajamas and a towel, and nearly bumped into He Jingting, who had just stepped out of the shower. His hair was still wet, and he was wearing a black silk long-sleeved pajama top; the V-neck hung loosely, partially revealing the firm muscles of his chest.

　

She quickly averted her gaze, her heart leaping into her throat: “Well, I’m going to take a shower.” The phrase ‘there’s no silver buried here’ seems to be appropriate in hinting at her clumsy attempt to hide her emotion.

As soon as she said it, she wanted to smack herself. Fortunately, He Jingting merely gave a faint “Mm-hmm” and walked past her into the living room to pour himself a glass of water.

Both the master bedroom and second bedroom have bathrooms, so Shu Cheng slipped into the shared one, which was closest. After closing the door, she pressed down on the latch and turned it very gently and slowly, locking it from the inside.

Shu Cheng let herself soak in the bathtub, staring blankly to pass the time, then buried her face in the water, blowing bubbles like a little fish.

Holding onto a sliver of hope after such an exhausting wedding banquet today—if he’s already asleep, does that mean she won’t have to spend her wedding night with him?

She soaked in the tub like that for a full hour, nearly passing out from the stuffiness, before cautiously making her way out.

The living room was pitch black, and no light seeped through the crack under the master bedroom door. Shu Cheng gently pushed the door open. In the dimly lit room, only a faint nightlight remained on.

Sure enough, He Jingting was already asleep. His eyes were tightly shut as he lay flat on the right side of the double bed, clearly fast asleep.

She slipped quietly under the covers on the other side, afraid of disturbing his peaceful sleep. Turning her back to him, she curled up, not daring to move a muscle. It was the first time in her life she’d shared a bed with a grown man.

Shu Cheng thought she’d lie awake all night, but an overwhelming wave of exhaustion washed over her, leaving her no time to overthink things as she drifted into a deep sleep.

However, just as her breathing began to steady—In the darkness, the man beside her slowly opened his eyes.

He Jingting’s eyes were clear as he quietly gazed at the girl’s sleeping silhouette. Her long, jet-black hair cascaded across the pillow, so soft that he couldn’t resist reaching out, his fingertips gently brushing the ends. Just that single touch sent a tingling sensation through his heart, leaving him unsettled for a long time.

The glass of water on the nightstand was already empty. He got out of bed, closed the door, went to the living room, poured himself another glass of cold water, and drank it all.

Just then, a faint fragrance, seemingly out of nowhere, wafted into his nostrils. He Jingting quickly pinpointed the source: the bathroom she had just used.

The window was slightly ajar; droplets of water slid down the fogged-up mirror. The air was warm and humid, faintly tinged with a sweet, fruity scent. His adam’s apple bobbed involuntarily twice as his gaze slowly swept over every extra item in the room.

A small bottle of women’s shampoo, a toothbrush standing beside the sink, and… A used bath towel hung on the towel rack, next to a pale pink hair dryer cap with two soft rabbit ears dangling from it.v

He Jingting’s eyes flickered, and he reached out to take the pink cap.

His fingertips sank into the soft, skin-friendly fleece; it felt damp, and the double-layered section still retained a hint of warmth.

The shampoo had a sweet peach scent so light and fragrant that it seemed to slightly soothe the pain that had not ceased all night, that had been forcibly suppressed by painkillers.

As if possessed, he buried the tip of his nose in it. An image of her after her bath involuntarily flashed through his mind—how her slender fingers would twist her wet hair into a bun, making her look as cute as if she’d grown a pair of little rabbit ears…

He Jingting’s breathing suddenly quickened, and a surge of restless heat rose from deep within his body. His slender fingers trembled, clenching the velvety fabric repeatedly until the last thread of his sanity snapped.

“Mmm…”

He let out a muffled groan, his eyes completely glazed over. It was unclear how long it took before He Jingting snapped out of his daze, breathing heavily.

Leaning against the sink, he closed his eyes tightly. He’d only caught a whiff of it. He bent down to turn on the faucet; the icy water gradually calmed him.

The shower cap was submerged in the water, scrubbed back and forth by a pair of large hands. The pungent mix of laundry detergent, soap, and disinfectant completely washed away the warm, pleasant scent that had filled the bathroom moments before. But in the end, it was neatly folded and tossed into the trash can.

The water in the sink kept flowing, carrying scattered bubbles as it disappeared into the drain. Drops of water trickled down his bulging neck, soaking a large patch of his collar.

He Jingting stared with disgust at his own ghostly pale face in the mirror.
