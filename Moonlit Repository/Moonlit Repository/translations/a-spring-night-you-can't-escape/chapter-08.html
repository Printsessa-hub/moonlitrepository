A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 08

Harsh

Shu Cheng blinked, thinking it must be a hallucination. Otherwise, why would He Jingting be here?

Under the pale corridor lights, he stood towering over her against the light, his shoulders blocking part of the glow and casting a patch of dappled shadows over her.

In the girl’s tear-filled eyes was a look of disbelief—almost as if she’d seen a ghost.

He Jingting called out softly, with a hint of resignation: “Shu Cheng.”

His deep, hoarse voice carried clearly to her ears through the thin mask. Shu Cheng unconsciously clenched her cuffs and shrank back into her seat.

It was real.

He Jingting said nothing more. He took the test report from her hand and sat down. The thin stack of papers turned slowly, page by page, between his slender fingers.

“You… you know,” Shu Cheng stammered. “My cat…”

He Jingting paused for a moment, then looked up and asked, “Didn’t you already ask Chen Yanqing about it?”

　　…

She was at a loss for words; it seemed her excuse about needing to buy painkillers hadn’t been very convincing.

“After the surgery, she’ll be transferred to Rui’an Hospital.” That was the best veterinary hospital in South City.

Shu Cheng heard him ask, “Have you eaten yet?”

She shook her head honestly. From that cup of latte she’d had that afternoon until now—past 8:00 p.m. She hadn’t had a single bite to eat.

Twenty minutes later, He Jingting’s assistant brought over an insulated bag.

Shu Cheng opened it, to her surprise, a box of exquisitely crafted shrimp dumplings and custard buns, along with a small bowl of steaming spare rib soup.

The warmth radiating through her palms felt so out of place in that moment that she held the food container motionless for a while.

“It’s not poisoned,” He Jingting said coldly, glancing her way. “If you don’t want to eat it, just throw it away.”

“…Thank you.” She murmured a soft thank you and picked up a shrimp dumpling with her chopsticks, popping it into her mouth.

The hot soup dispelled the chill of the autumn night, warming her body, and her nerves—which had been on edge for so long—seemed to relax just a little.

He Jingting, however, showed no intention of picking up his chopsticks. His brow was slightly furrowed as he handled some business on his phone. The soft white glow from the screen cast a faint light on his focused profile. The moment he frowned, his aura suddenly turned sharp and imposing, making everyone afraid to speak.

But since he was the one who had bought them, Shu Cheng asked softly: “Do you want some?”

He Jingting waved his hand. As he turned his head, his knuckles pressed against his mask, and he couldn’t help but cough lightly twice.

Since entering the veterinary clinic, he had been coughing intermittently. Though the coughs weren’t severe, one could still sense his discomfort with the stale air.

“Are you okay?” Shu Cheng couldn’t help but sound a little worried. “This place is full of animal fur. Why don’t you go back to work first? I can handle this on my own…”

Before she could finish, her phone began to vibrate repeatedly.

He Jingting ignored her suggestion, pulled a Bluetooth headset from his pocket, and put it on. As he joined the conference call, he stood up and walked toward a quieter part of the hallway.

At the end of the hallway, he stood by a half-open window, his silhouette blurred in the dim light. Even though he was quite far away, she could almost hear his steady, confident voice as he negotiated.

The sweet filling melted on her tongue as Shu Cheng took small bites of the custard bun, her mind drifting off.

He Jingting was waiting with her for the kitten’s surgery… Why did it feel so unreal?

Half an hour later, the surgery was concluded successfully. The kitten was out of danger and was immediately transferred to Rui’an Hospital, which had been arranged in advance.

There’s a time difference between Germany and here, and ever since the transfer began, He Jingting’s phone hadn’t stopped ringing. Whatever had prompted him to personally handle this in the middle of the night couldn’t be a minor matter, so Shu Cheng quietly stayed by his side, not wanting to disturb him.

After the examination, the doctor arranged for three days of hospitalization for observation and IV treatment. Fortunately, the medical environment and facilities here were reassuring.

Through the glass window of the intensive care unit, Shu Cheng watched with concern as Tuantuan lay hooked up to an IV. The kitten, all fluffy, was curled up into a tiny ball, still under the effects of the anesthesia and sleeping soundly.

Suddenly, someone yanked her forearm forcefully to the right—A nursing cart rushed past, grazing the hem of her coat; it had come within a hair’s breadth of hitting her.

Shu Cheng stumbled half a step, her nose nearly brushing against the chest of the man beside her, and she subconsciously looked up.

In the quiet, empty hallway, He Jingting held her hand tightly without letting go. He was already a head taller than her, and now, standing before her against the light, he exuded an indescribable sense of oppression.

In that moment, Shu Cheng even forgot to step back.

He Jingting frowned at first. The temperature had dropped sharply in the late autumn midnight, and through the thin layer of her knitted sweater, the girl’s slightly cool body temperature seeped into the palm of his hand.

The sound of the meeting report continued to flow steadily through his headphones.

“I want to hear solutions, not a repeat of the financials. Skip this part.”

He Jingting interrupted, taking off his trench coat and draping it over her shoulders.

The gesture carried a hint of undeniable forcefulness. He gripped the collar with one hand while his other arm wrapped around her shoulders from behind. As he leaned in, that faint scent of sandalwood drifted toward her once more.

For a moment, it felt as though he had drawn her into his embrace. Shu Cheng froze in place, her heart skipping a beat.

With a deft flick of his long fingers, he fastened the second button.

The oversized trench coat wrapped her securely, carrying his body heat and completely blocking out the chill of the autumn night.

She stammered, “I’m not cold…”

Before she could finish, He Jingting tapped his headset twice, then placed his index finger to his lips, signaling her to be quiet.

The conference call with the German headquarters was still in progress.

“Eight percent, that’s our bottom line.”

He turned his head to speak; in the dim light of the intensive care unit, his sharply defined jawline was slightly tense, exuding a stern aura.

Shu Cheng obediently remained silent, her fingertips gently tugging at the collar to pull it tighter.

By the time they left Rui’an Hospital, it was already past 1:00 a.m. Secretary Zhong was out, so a middle-aged driver came to pick them up.

On the way back, He Jingting was still working; the faint glow of his Bluetooth headset flickered on and off in the darkness.

The last time she rode with him was the night after the wedding. But for some reason, even though less than half a month had passed, Shu Cheng no longer felt that excessive unease that had made her want to press herself against the window to put some distance between them.

The journey back was long, and the black sedan sped along the empty highway.

After a long, exhausting day, she was truly worn out to the bone. The gentle murmur beside her was like a soothing white noise; wrapped in his trench coat, Shu Cheng actually drifted off to sleep without even realizing it.

As the car bumped along, the seat was soft, and her head slid down several times, yet she was too sleepy to open her eyes.

In her daze, a pair of arms pulled her into his embrace. The suit fabric felt cool to the touch, and leaning against it was comforting.

A few strands of hair lay scattered around her face, feeling slightly sticky, and were gently brushed aside by a cool fingertip… Shu Cheng instinctively nuzzled against him and drifted off to sleep.

She slept soundly, and when she awoke again, the car had come to a stop, and the surroundings were unusually quiet.

Shu Cheng raised her eyes dazedly and suddenly met a pair of calm, dark eyes.

The man’s gaze was half-hidden in the dim light as he stared at her motionlessly. The side windows had all been thoughtfully covered with blackout shades; only a sliver of the garage’s cold, white light filtered through the windshield, reflecting in his deep pupils.

That gaze was unfamiliar, seemingly filled with intense emotions she couldn’t decipher, like a dangerous, turbulent underground river, ready to suck her in.

After locking eyes for a few seconds, Shu Cheng found herself momentarily stunned.

He Jingting’s thin lips parted slightly: “Awake?”

Only then did she realize she’d actually fallen asleep leaning against his shoulder.

The driver’s seat was empty; the driver had long since left, and she had no idea how long she’d been asleep like that.

“You… why didn’t you wake me…” She sat up awkwardly, noticing that the crisp shoulders of his trench coat were wrinkled—thankfully, there was no drool.

“We just arrived.”

He didn’t look at her again and got out of the car without a word. That strange look in his eyes from earlier had vanished in an instant, as if it had been a mere illusion.

Shu Cheng followed silently. Once inside, He Jingting sat down on the sofa and opened his laptop, seemingly intent on continuing his work.

The clock in the living room had already struck three in the morning. Did it really take this long to drive back from Rui’an Hospital?

“Then, I’ll go to sleep first.” She couldn’t help but yawn; her eyes were a little red from sleepiness.

“Hmmm.” He Jingting’s gaze lingered for a few seconds, then he suddenly called out to her, “Set aside some time next Saturday. He Zhengyuan’s birthday banquet, you’ll attend with me.”

He Zhengyuan?

Shu Cheng took a few seconds to realize he was referring to Old Mr. He, his father.

She nodded in agreement, then turned to the bathroom to wash up, asking casually: “By the way… have you seen my hair cap?”

It had been hanging there just fine, but now it was gone.

The sound of He Jingting typing on the keyboard stopped. “There are new ones in the cabinet.”

Sure enough, Shu Cheng found a brand new one in the cabinet above the sink. It was also light pink and very similar to the previous one. It was fluffy and thick, and seemed to be of even better quality.

“What about the old one?”

“It got dirty.” He seemed to think of something, and his Adam’s apple bobbed slightly. “It fell on the floor, so I threw it away.”

She was puzzled. Couldn’t he have just picked it up, washed it, and kept using it?

But He Jingting lowered his head to put on his headphones, as if he were about to take a call, showing no intention of continuing the conversation.

　　*

A cold rain fell sporadically on the branches and leaves, and the temperature dropped sharply; autumn was left with only a short tail.

On the day the kitten was discharged from the hospital, Shu Cheng brought it to Jiang Yuan’s house.

“Didn’t He Jingting agree to let you keep a cat at home?”

Jiang Yuan tried to pet Tuantuan’s back, but the kitten was still a bit wary of the unfamiliar surroundings and kept backing away.

“Let it sniff your hand first; it’ll be better once it gets used to you.” Shu Cheng gently cradled the kitten in her arms, brought her finger close to its nose, and replied vaguely, “Mm, it’s not really convenient to take it home right now.”

That night, He Jingting’s attitude toward the kitten had been relatively gentle, but even with a mask on at the hospital, he’d been coughing constantly—clearly not feeling well. Since he hadn’t brought it up again afterward, she assumed he wouldn’t agree.

He’d already gone out of his way to help arrange the hospital visit; she couldn’t push him any further.

“With a house that big, and it’s not like he has to clean it, is there really no room for such an adorable little kitten? ” Jiang Yuan said indignantly, “He was so fierce the last time we met, he really is hard to get along with.”

Shu Cheng smiled a little sheepishly: “Actually, it’s not that bad…”

For a leader like He Jingting, his health condition is always kept confidential for the sake of the group’s interests. Only those closest to him know he suffers from asthma, so she couldn’t reveal the real reason.

“What do you mean, ‘it’s actually not that bad’? He must be bullying people behind the scenes all the time.” Jiang Yuan had just dyed her long, curly hair a bright purple, looking gorgeous and alluring, and her exaggerated expression made her look incredibly lively.

“You have to tell me about it. Even though I can’t do anything to him, I can secretly go and let all the air out of his car tires!”

“Okay, if anything comes up, you’ll be the first to know.” Shu Cheng was amused by her and changed the subject. “Ever since you started dating, why has your whole style changed?”

Jiang Yuan had just started dating a guy in a band. She’d completely transformed her usual smoky eyes, a bright pink American-style tank top, and a ripped sweater that offered almost no warmth, transforming into a cool, sassy girl.

She grinned and held out her colorful manicured nails: “I’m keeping up with the trends for love! Don’t they look great?”

“They look great,” Shu Cheng couldn’t help but chuckle. “But I bet your dad doesn’t know about this, at least he hasn’t called me yet.”

“I couldn’t care less about him,” Jiang Yuan said nonchalantly. “Anyway, when the time comes, I’ll marry whoever he tells me to. But until then, I’m going to enjoy dating to the fullest!”

Jiang Yuan was the youngest daughter in her family. Her older sister had been forced into marriage at a young age, so from childhood she had seen through the marriages driven by fame and fortune, vowing to get her fill of romance before stepping into that “grave.”

Ever since college, she’d been constantly switching boyfriends—passionate when in love, wailing when they broke up. Her love stories could fill a whole legend…

“He’s performing at a music festival early next month. The live show is going to be absolutely electrifying. You absolutely have to come!” Jiang Yuan beamed with joy as she excitedly pulled out a poster to show her friend. “Look, he’s definitely the hottest guy in the band, right?”

Shu Cheng watched her best friend’s animated expression as she talked about her boyfriend and couldn’t help but feel a twinge of envy for her carefree and unrestrained nature.

Such passionate love was something she would never experience in this lifetime.

Just then, her phone rang. Seeing “He Jingting” on the screen made Shu Cheng’s heart skip a beat.

Since they’d been married, he hadn’t once called her directly. She felt a little nervous: “Hello?”

On the other end, He Jingting’s voice was deep and magnetic, tinged with a faint static that made it sound somewhat unreal: “Where are you?”

“At a friend’s house.”

His questions were always to the point: “When will you be back?”

Shu Cheng glanced at her watch; it was nearly 8:00 p.m. Did he need something? “I’ll be back soon. Do you need me…”

He interrupted her casually: “Send me the address.”

After she hung up, Jiang Yuan noticed the complicated expression on her face and asked curiously, “Who was that so late at night?”

“I…”

Shu Cheng paused. “My husband” sounded so cheesy; she just couldn’t bring herself to say it.

She licked her dry lips and decided to just call him by his name: “He Jingting said he’s coming to pick me up and take me home.”

Jiang Yuan was taken aback. “Oh? Why?”

In all the time they’d been married, she’d never seen a couple so inseparable.

“……” Actually, that was exactly what she wanted to ask.

Twenty minutes later, Shu Cheng hugged the kitten again and again, repeatedly reminding it to be fed treats every day; Shu Cheng reluctantly headed downstairs.

An unfamiliar black Maybach was already parked in the night, its glaring red taillights illuminated. As she approached, it remained completely still. Amid the swaying shadows of the trees, it resembled a predator lurking in the darkness, ready to pounce at any moment.

He Jingting’s cars were usually black or dark in color. Shu Cheng walked over and tentatively pulled open the door.

The backseat was empty, and the soft interior lighting cast the silhouette of a man in the driver’s seat. To her surprise, He Jingting was driving himself.

Naturally, Shu Cheng didn’t dare treat him as a driver; she obediently slipped back into the passenger seat.

He Jingting started the car immediately; the ambient lights dimmed, and the interior fell into silence, broken only by the faint hum of the air conditioner’s heater.

He suddenly asked, “Where’s your ring?”

“I left it at home,” Shu Cheng said, touching her empty ring finger. “I wash my hands so often at work—it’s easy to lose.”

That wedding ring was an extremely rare, flawless pink diamond weighing a full five carats and worth at least a million. She simply couldn’t bear to let it get stained by the lead dust and debris in her studio.

“Wear it. If you lose it, we’ll just buy another one,” He Jingting said indifferently. “If you take off your ring right after getting married, people will think there’s trouble in our relationship.”

Their relationship? Shu Cheng paused for a moment, then looked at him.

But the man’s expression remained calm, as if he’d just said something utterly ordinary. His left hand rested casually on the steering wheel, his wedding ring adorning his well-defined knuckles and powerful hand, exuding an indescribable sensuality.

She didn’t dare look any longer and averted her gaze: “I understand…”

The front seats offered a more intimate space; there was no room to move about, and the two were so close they could almost hear each other’s breathing.

For the first time, Shu Cheng found herself missing Secretary Zhong’s driving, so she quietly rolled down the window a little.

The cool autumn night breeze swept in, slightly easing the invisible stifling atmosphere, but He Jingting seemed a bit uncomfortable and covered his mouth to cough a few times.

No sooner had she rolled the window back up than she heard him ask, “Is your cat being discharged from the hospital today?”

He raised his hand and turned the air conditioning down by two degrees.

“Yes, it’s been settled in. It’ll be staying at a friend’s house for now,” Shu Cheng said obediently. “You met her at the bridal shop, Jiang Yuan.”

He Jingting said nothing. He accelerated slightly, turned the car around, and drove onto the elevated highway. His expression was impassive; it was impossible to tell if he had any reservations about her answer.

She quickly added, “Don’t worry, I definitely won’t bring the cat back.”

The car sped along the empty elevated highway, the wind howling past them.

The girl beside him looked serious, her pink lips pressed together lightly, appearing docile and obedient. Yet, in every word she spoke, there were only “you” and “I,” not a single mention of “us,” which sounded so jarring.

He was automatically excluded from everything and everyone important to her.

He Jingting’s arm gripping the steering wheel tensed slightly, betraying his suppressed displeasure: “Don’t you really like this cat? Are you just going to leave it at a friend’s house?”

“Jiang Yuan really likes Tuantuan, and besides, isn’t it… impossible to keep a cat at home?” Sensing his aura suddenly grow heavier, Shu Cheng’s voice grew softer and softer.

He Jingting frowned: “Did you ask me?” The hoarse, slightly upturned tone at the end of his sentence struck a chord in her heart.

“……”

He was used to being in control of everything and likely wouldn’t tolerate decisions made without his knowledge, including hers. As his wife, she was essentially his possession.

Shu Cheng said softly, “Oh, then from now on… I’ll ask for your opinion first.”

But the gloom on He Jingting’s face didn’t dissipate because of this concession. He accelerated rapidly, overtaking several cars traveling alongside them in a few quick moves as he sped ahead, the engine roaring continuously.

Shu Cheng stole a glance at him; his expression was as cold as ice.

Did he really expect her to admit she was wrong and say “I’m sorry”? She hadn’t caused him any trouble at all; this was just too much! She shrank back into her seat and remained silent.

Twenty minutes later, the Maybach pulled up in front of the main gate of Yujiang Mansion. He Jingting pulled over to the side of the road and turned off the engine, showing no intention of turning into the underground parking garage.

After a moment of tense silence, he asked, “Do you need me to ask you to get out of the car?”

Another sarcastic remark.

He either spoke in imperatives or interrogative sentences; she disliked it, but didn’t want to argue with him either.

“Aren’t you going back?”

Her voice remained as gentle as ever. The light from the street lights fell diagonally across the car window. Casting a hazy, warm yellow glow that reflected a thin layer of light in her beautiful eyes.

He Jingting’s gaze lingered for a few seconds. “I’m going on a business trip for a few days. I’m heading to the airport.”

“Then what about the birthday banquet on Saturday night…”

“I’ll come back early.” He paused, then suddenly asked, “Why aren’t you using the card I gave you?”

Ever since he’d given it to her, there had been no spending notifications for that supplementary card.

Shu Cheng answered honestly, “We haven’t bought much for the house.”

Her design fees were quite high, more than enough to cover her own expenses—and with no joint marital expenses lately, there was no reason to use the supplementary card.

“I told you, you should use this card for all your purchases.”

He Jingting’s tone left no room for negotiation.

Shu Cheng wanted to argue, but remembering their earlier disagreement, she hesitated for a moment before nodding. “Okay.”

She habitually avoided conflict. At least when she was a child, this tactic had worked well—adults were busy and didn’t really care; they’d probably forget about it in a couple of days.

“Get some rest.” He Jingting’s curt remark brought the conversation to a complete end.

Even after getting out of the car and walking home along the path, Shu Cheng still felt a bit dazed.

Since he was going on a business trip, why had he suddenly come to pick her up? Could it be that he’d come specifically to say goodbye to her?

As this thought crossed her mind, a gust of autumn wind sent a shiver down her spine. Impossible.

The girl’s silhouette gradually faded into the night, yet long after, the Maybach parked at the entrance still hadn’t driven away.

He Jingting stared motionless ahead, his pupils as black as ink. Amid the bright lights of the Yujiang Mansion, a window on the top floor had lit up at some point.

Suddenly, his phone vibrated twice, and a message popped up on the screen.

Lobby Manager: [Mr. He, here are the blueprints and plans for converting several secondary bedrooms into pet rooms. Please review them. Construction can begin as early as the day after tomorrow.]

The previous entry in the list was from Chen Yanqing: [Don’t you know how your health is? Are you really going to bring a cat home? Are you kidding me?]

In the darkness, the glow from the screen cast a light on the man’s pale face.

He Jingting’s sharp eyes dimmed slightly. His chest heaving, his breathing heavy, as if he were struggling to suppress something. The knuckles of his finger pressing the delete button turned white, and then he slammed the phone down on the center console with a loud “thud.”

After a long moment, he leaned back weakly in his seat, closed his eyes, and exhaled softly—as if letting out a helpless sigh.
