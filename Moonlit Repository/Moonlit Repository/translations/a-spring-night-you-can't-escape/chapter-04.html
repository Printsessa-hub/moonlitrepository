A SPRING NIGHT YOU CAN'T ESCAPE · Chapter 04

Struggle

Shu Cheng felt as if she were nailed to the spot; she instinctively held her breath and, overcome with guilt, couldn’t utter a single word.

As for the excuse about being on a business trip—if he wanted to, he could expose it with just a flick of his finger.

He Jingting stared intently at her, his grip not loosening in the slightest: “You’d better always remember that we are legally bound. You should fulfill your duties as a wife, not going off to meet with other men in secret.”

Secret meetings? Shu Cheng froze: “I didn’t…”

Before she could finish, He Jingting suddenly took a long stride forward, his presence bearing down on her, and said coldly: “Do you need me to remind you where you were on the night of the 6th?”

Shu Cheng instinctively recoiled, her back quickly pressing against the hard wall, leaving her with nowhere to escape.

The icy chill against her back snapped her back to reality. The night of the 6th—that was the very night she’d left the studio and had dinner with Lu Siyan at that private Cantonese restaurant.

He Jingting actually knew all about that!

“You were following me?” She felt a chill run down her spine.

He could look down on the Shu family, but he had no right to insult her character.

“Lu Siyan is just a friend of mine. Besides, we only met because you went and announced our engagement without my consent…” A hint of fear flashed in Shu Cheng’s eyes, but she forced herself to finish her sentence in one breath, gasping slightly. “You dragged us both into this media storm.”

The moment those first three words left her lips, He Jingting’s expression had already turned completely cold.

Thunder roared outside the window, rumbling across the sky.

He stared at the petite, delicate girl before him—the same girl who had been trembling with fear just a minute ago, yet in the blink of an eye was so fiercely defending another man.

She avoided him like the plague, yet the moment she turned her head, she was chatting happily with that man surnamed Lu!

He Jingting was so consumed by jealousy he felt like he was going mad; his heart pounded so hard in his left chest it felt as if it might burst, triggering waves of intense suffocation. His jaw tightened slightly, like an eerie calm before a violent storm.

His voice was hoarse, laced with a hint of suppressed anger: “You’d better think carefully about what kind of relationship you have with him, and with me.”

Meeting his dark, burning gaze, Shu Cheng’s heart skipped a beat. A sense of foreboding washed over her, and she instinctively wanted to run away.

But it was already too late.

He Jingting suddenly grabbed her wrist and pressed her hard against the wall behind her. Her wrist bone slammed against the hard surface, and Shu Cheng winced in pain.

“You didn’t dare sit next to me, you’ve been avoiding me… Are you that afraid of me? Why?”

He stared intently at her crimson lips, closing in on her inch by inch: “You like him. You regret marrying me, don’t you?”

His frustration had already caused He Jingting to lose his patience; he desperately sought an answer.

Even if it were just an excuse—or a lie.

“Answer me!”

Shu Cheng was no longer capable of thinking; she struggled desperately. But her strength was as feeble as an ant trying to shake a tree; she was firmly trapped, unable to move, shrouded in the shadow he cast over her.

In this moment, fragments of memories long buried deep within her mind surged to the surface.

“No, no!”

Overcome by intense fear, she instinctively cried out the name she had used for him in her youth.

“Big Brother…”

Those two trembling words pierced He Jingting’s heart like a sharp arrow. His bloodshot pupils trembled, and his mind suddenly cleared.

Shu Cheng struggled, and in the brief moment his grip weakened, she instantly broke free from his tight hold.

Her elbow, flailing wildly, slammed hard into He Jingting’s chest. He let out a muffled groan, and the oppressive force vanished completely.

With no time to think of anything else, and terrified of being caught again, she fled as fast as she could.

In the dimly lit living room, He Jingting clutched his chest with one hand, his upper body bent deeply forward as his gasps grew heavier with each breath. Even so, he forced himself to lift his head, trying through blurred vision to catch one last glimpse of the fleeing figure.

The room fell into complete silence. He staggered and knelt unsteadily on the floor, his chest emitting intermittent, choking sounds.

For a few minutes after taking his medication, as he fought off the dizziness, the faint redness at the corners of her eyes kept flashing before He Jingting’s eyes.

It was his own impulsiveness that had hurt her.

The tightness in his chest only grew with his regret; his clenched fists trembled, and cold sweat instantly soaked the collar of his shirt.

Suddenly, his cell phone rang abruptly.

He answered; it was his secretary, relaying the message with some urgency.

“Got it,” He Jingting said, unable to suppress a low cough. “Book me a flight right now…”

The clock on the wall slowly crept toward the number 12 a.m. In the boundless darkness of the night, the call ended, and his leaning figure remained still for a moment.

Even though no one else was in the room, he still struggled to straighten his back—as if stubbornly holding onto something—and rushed out the door.

　*

Meanwhile, Shu Cheng drove out of Yujiang Mansion in the pouring rain.

In the middle of the night, amid the torrential rain, the windshield wipers swung back and forth, and her hands still trembled slightly as she gripped the steering wheel. The lingering tension had not yet subsided, so she naturally failed to notice that a black Bentley was following closely behind her, on the empty road.

The red taillights vanished into the rain as the warm light in the bedroom on the sixteenth floor illuminated the room.

After returning to her apartment, Shu Cheng took a hot shower, then sat on the bay window with Tuantuan in her arms, staring blankly as raindrops fell down the glass, blurring the city’s neon lights.

She recalled the man’s face as he had closed in on her just moments ago, and the look in his eyes—as if he wanted to tear her to pieces…

A complex mix of emotions welled up inside her: fear, confusion, and a faint sense of absurdity.

In her childhood memories, He Jingting always seemed to be surrounded by a cold and chilling aura.

The door to the room at the far end of the third floor was always tightly shut. He only came downstairs occasionally for dinner; most of the time, because he suffered from asthma and was sensitive to many foods, his meals were prepared separately and brought to his room.

He was a full five years older than her. A boy of distinguished background, he was handsome yet aloof and taciturn—a figure who inspired awe, thus adding an air of mystery.v

Little Shucheng, who had just started middle school, felt a mixture of fear and curiosity towards this “big brother.” She had once secretly peeked through the crack in his door to watch his back.

But the incident that occurred when she was fourteen was what truly made her avoid him at all costs from then on.

That winter, Old Master He celebrated his 70th birthday while ill. Perhaps because “a dying man speaks the truth,” or perhaps to atone for his sins, he remembered this grandson—who had been living in exile for over a decade and whom he had mistreated and directly invited him to the banquet.

This was a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity, effectively amounting to the He family publicly acknowledging him as their own. But when it came to inheriting the family estate—the He family already had a young grandson—how could his legally wedded daughter-in-law possibly agree? She threw a huge fit at the He residence.

However, on the very afternoon of the birthday banquet, He Jingting accidentally fell down the stairs, suffering a fracture of his left wrist. He was rushed to the hospital for emergency surgery, and just like that, he missed his chance to “change his fate.”

At the time, onlookers all lamented: “This child is simply not destined for good fortune.”

Only Shu Cheng knew that this was not the truth.

That evening, in the deep twilight, she was in the second-floor hallway, bending down to pick up a pearl that had fallen from the hem of her skirt…

With her own eyes, she saw that gaunt teenager standing in the dim light, slowly wedging his left wrist into the gap between the spiral railings, turning it a few times as if searching for the right angle.

Then, without batting an eye, he “missed a step” and tumbled down from the top of the stairs, crashing heavily onto the floor.

There was a loud “thud”—

The butler, who had been changing the candlesticks in the living room, came running over with a startled cry. Daring not to make a single wrong move, he rushed to get the phone—not to call an ambulance, but to report the incident to Mr. Shu and await further instructions.

On the glossy mahogany floor, He Jingting slowly sat up. His face was pale, and cold sweat from the intense pain drenched his disheveled hair, yet his expression was utterly cold and showed no sign of pain.

His left hand was bent at an unnatural angle, the fingertips dangling limply as if severed.

As soon as the butler turned away, he raised his right hand to grasp the injured limb, clamping down on the wound and ruthlessly bending it back and forth with all his strength.

The scene was horrifying. Little Shu Cheng was so terrified she fell silent, her heart seeming to stop beating for a moment. This little girl, who would cry with reddened eyes for hours over even a minor scrape on her knee during gym class, had never witnessed such a terrifying act.

Her fingertips trembled, and the bead she’d picked up in her palm slipped from her grasp, falling with a “plop” and clattering onto the corridor floor.

The sound was faint, but in that moment of silence, it reached the boy’s ears with unparalleled clarity.

He Jingting slowly raised his head, his piercing gaze piercing through the railing to meet Shu Cheng’s terrified eyes from afar. His deep, dark eyes darkened, becoming dangerous and oppressive—like a tiger ready to tear a lamb apart and drink its blood at any moment.

Then, he raised his uninjured right hand and placed his index finger to his lips.

Shh. He knew she had seen him.

A cold sweat broke out on Shu Cheng’s back; she moved her stiff legs and fled back to her room as fast as she could.

That night, she had nightmares all night long, repeatedly dreaming of He Jingting snapping his left wrist and walking toward her, holding up his blood-soaked hand.

Upon waking up, Shu Cheng huddled under her blanket, trembling uncontrollably—she had glimpsed something she dared not look at. He Jingting was so ruthless; would he secretly have her killed?

That scene became a psychological trauma for the young girl; her curiosity vanished completely, leaving only fear.

From then on, she never dared to meet He Jingting’s gaze again, always keeping her distance. Whenever she heard a sound coming from the third floor, she wouldn’t even dare to open her door…

Ten years later, Shu Cheng finally understood the strategy of “retreating to advance”—lulling one’s opponent into a false sense of security before delivering a fatal blow.

And that cold, piercing gaze of the boy in the dim hallway had haunted her nightmares ever since she was a child.

　*

Over the next few days, Shu Cheng deliberately stayed home, terrified of any further encounter with He Jingting.

With the wedding approaching, there were numerous minor details to confirm, but she used illness as an excuse to turn down every call from the staff, telling them to contact He Jingting instead.

It wasn’t until Thursday afternoon that the bridal shop invited her to try on her custom-made gown.

Since no one could take her place for this, Shu Cheng asked indirectly, “Has President He tried on his suit yet?

“Rest assured, Mr. He’s suit has already been delivered to Beichuan by a dedicated courier.”

So he wasn’t in Nanshi after all. Shu Cheng breathed a sigh of relief and gladly agreed to try on the gown in person.

The VIP lounge was just as meticulous and attentive as last time, but neither the designer nor the manager attending to her were the familiar faces from before, and the atmosphere felt a bit awkward.

A young waitress accidentally spilled a few drops of coffee while serving refreshments. She glanced at Shu Cheng in a panic, her hands shaking so much she could barely hold the napkin, and stammered, “I’m so sorry, Mrs. He! I’m so sorry! I’ll get you a new cup right away!”

“It’s fine, no need to replace it.”

Shu Cheng felt inexplicably uncomfortable, so she hurried through the wedding dress fitting and politely took her leave.

The roses in the courtyard were still in full bloom, their fragrance wafting on the autumn breeze.

Just as she stepped out of the courtyard gate, ready to text Jiang Yuan to arrange lunch, two people suddenly burst out from the bushes nearby.

A man and a woman, standing three steps away, dropped to their knees with a thud.

“Mrs. He, we’re so sorry—we were in the wrong! Please, please ask Mr. He to have mercy on us and let us off!”

The woman, in her early thirties with disheveled long hair, was pulling the man down as she repeatedly bowed her head to the ground.

Shu Cheng had never witnessed such a scene before; startled, she took two steps back. “What’s going on?”

The security guard nearby, seeing this, immediately rushed over and pinned the two down. “Didn’t we warn you that we’d call the police if you didn’t leave? Who gave you the audacity to disturb Mrs. He?”

The woman lifted her ashen face, and upon closer inspection, it turned out to be the manager who had helped her try on wedding gowns last time.

In less than half a month, that once elegant and slender woman in high heels had become so haggard she was unrecognizable; her almond-shaped eyes were swollen and uneven. The man beside her wasn’t much better off—one of his feet was encased in a thick cast.

Shu Cheng froze for a moment: “Wait… Manager Li, what happened?”

“My mother has late-stage lung cancer. Every round of chemotherapy costs money. I was truly at my wits’ end, that’s why I momentarily lost my senses and believed his lies!” Manager Li struggled to get closer and shoved a stack of hospital reports into her hands. “Our lives right now are worse than death…”

From her disjointed, heart-wrenching cries, Shu Cheng pieced together the story.

The marriage alliance between the He and Shu families involved trade secrets, and all staff involved in the wedding preparations had been rigorously screened and had signed non-disclosure agreements.

But after the last wedding dress fitting, Manager Li and her boyfriend, a journalist, were overcome by greed. To pay for her mother’s medical treatment, the two sold the inside story of this high-society marriage…

Shu Cheng was stunned. So, it wasn’t He Jingting who had broken his promise and announced the engagement without authorization.

“Mrs. He, I truly realize my mistake. Please spare us! We promise to return to our hometown and never set foot in Nanshi again for the rest of our lives!”

The thin hospital reports scattered across the floor in the breeze. Given He Jingting’s ruthless methods, they had surely already suffered a hundred or a thousand times the punishment they deserved for this mistake.

Watching Manager Li and her boyfriend being dragged away in disgrace by the security guards, Shu Cheng couldn’t help but feel a pang of sympathy.

But she was far from “Mrs. He” whom the world saw as the object of everyone’s adoration; she, too, was merely a clay bodhisattva crossing the river.

In the blink of an eye, the ginkgo trees were laden with leaves, and the wedding was just around the corner.

The wedding was held at Nanshi’s most luxurious lakeside villa. As night fell, the Crystal Ballroom shone in resplendent splendor, like a dazzling pearl in a dark river.

Rather than a wedding, it felt more like a business gathering of the elite.

Shu Cheng waited in a private dressing room on the third floor. She’d been getting ready since noon and had been sitting upright in her voluminous, opulent wedding gown for several hours. Even before the ceremony had officially begun, her back and legs were already aching.

Fortunately, Jiang Yuan was there to keep her company; the two chatted and joked around, so it wasn’t too boring. After a while, the butler knocked lightly on the door to remind her: “Mr. He is already in the lounge.”
